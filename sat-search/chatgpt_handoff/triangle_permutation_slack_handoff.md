# Triangle frontier stability: permutation signatures and factor-information slack

Date: 2026-11-26. Starting master f162604f0a39d17dff004f020159eb2c559982a3. Extends triangle_frontier_rigidity_handoff.md and triangle_incidence_cut_handoff.md. New unrestricted Boolean-map capacity theorem for near-minimum-width frontiers retaining individual-factor information, including early Z ingress, entropy and matching bounds for raw bypass coordinates, and a counterexample to port-identity stability. Paper proofs and finite regression only; no independent audit, Lean proof, production changes or unrestricted superlinear SAT gate bound. L-clb-036 remains open.

## 1. Setup and permutation signature theorem

Reuse the exact fixed-coordinate triangle SAT projection, word length n=Theta(A^2 log A). N=A^2, P=XY Boolean product, and transpose Z when identifying it with an N-bit query mask; output is ND(P,Z)=1 iff P intersects Z. Here P denotes the product matrix, not the earlier dictionary-size parameter. All 2^N products occur. Work with the topological-prefix and data-frontier conventions of triangle_frontier_rigidity_handoff.md. A frontier of c data-dependent signals, with q distinct data-free Z-dependent ingress wires, determines a map E_a(X,Y) for each code a(Z). Other original coordinates are common fixed constants. No assumption of monotonicity on any map or gate, no efficiency assumption on the decoder.

First set q=0, so E does not depend on Z. Equal codes imply equal products by the singleton Z queries. Suppose a subset of the frontier signals depends only on X (may include raw X primaries bypassing the prefix, or arbitrary functions of X). Define its signature S_X(X), and M_X=number of distinct S_X(Pi) for A-by-A permutation matrices Pi. For every product P and permutation Pi,
    X=Pi, Y=Pi^{-1} P
is a valid Boolean factorization. Thus for EVERY product the set of attainable frontier codes has size at least M_X, since the specified factor-only port values are part of the code. The same argument using Y=Pi, X=P Pi^{-1} gives M_Y for any Y-only port set. Product fibers have disjoint code sets. Consequently
    2^c >= 2^N max(M_X,M_Y).
Write c=N+k. Then
    k >= ceil(log2 max(M_X,M_Y)).
Do NOT multiply M_X and M_Y: the same factorization need not realize arbitrary independent signatures on both sides. A factor-only port may be nonconstant on general matrices but constant on permutations; the theorem does not bound its number by k in that case. This works with nonmonotone encoders, signed AIGs, arbitrary proper B2 gates and even arbitrary Boolean maps. It is stronger than a count of raw ports because it can apply to derived pure-factor signals. It does not imply that every near-minimum code is product-invariant.

## 2. Early Z ingress, multiplicative cell capacity

For each nonempty code cell H_a={Z:a(Z)=a}, let r_a be the number of distinct ND truth-pattern vectors on all possible product masks, indexed by Z in H_a. Each pattern is realized by some product P. All 2^N products occur and for each such P all M_X signatures occur. If the specified X-only port tuple is fixed independently of Z (in particular if the tuple consists of raw bypass X bits), then E_a needs at least r_a M_X different codes: choose one product per pattern, and within it all permutation signatures. Two codes for distinct patterns cannot coincide, since some Z in the cell distinguishes them. The same for Y, use M=max(M_X,M_Y). Hence r_a<=C=floor(2^c/M).
From the previous disjointness cell lemma r_a>=|H_a|, and r_a>=|H_a|+1 for cells not containing Z=0. Summing over at most 2^q cells gives
    2^N <= 2^q*(floor(2^c/M)-1)+1.
This includes the earlier exact capacity inequality when M=1. It implies c+q>=N+ceil(log2 M), and if M is a power of two and q>0, c+q>=N+log2 M+1 (strict deficit). For q=0 it recovers Section 1. This is a necessary inequality, NOT asserted exact for a specified retained factor tuple, since other factorizations may supply additional signatures and the actual E may encode more. The same per-cell product bound holds with M_a=max(M_X(a),M_Y(a)) when pure-factor port functions may depend on the fixed ingress code; sum the individual capacities floor(2^c/M_a), subtract one for each nonzero-only cell. Do not infer M_a from another code. This is the promised early-mixing extension, still requiring the topological data-prefix decomposition.

## 3. Raw bypass coordinates: matching and entropy

Let R be any subset of A^2 raw X coordinates retained directly at the frontier, r=|R|. Their permutation signature is the intersection of R with the permutation's perfect matching in the complete bipartite graph. Let M(R) count distinct such patterns.

Matching bound: if R contains d disjoint row/column edges, choose a matching of size d'=min(d,A-1). Every subset of these d' specified positions can be present in a permutation while all remaining specified positions absent. To see this, extend the chosen subset by a permutation on the remaining rows and columns avoiding the disjoint forbidden positions. At least one remaining row/column is not one of the specified positions. If there are any forbidden positions, the remaining dimension is at least two and a cyclic shift after aligning forbidden positions avoids them. If none, any extension works. Thus M(R)>=2^d'. If q=0 and k<A-1, Section 1 implies maximum matching d<=k. By the bipartite matching/vertex-cover theorem, all retained raw positions are covered by at most k rows and/or columns. This is a structural statement on the coordinate pattern of bypassed inputs, not just their count.

Entropy bound: let Pi be a uniformly random permutation, and W be its raw projection onto R. Write d_i for number of retained coordinates in row i. Reveal the projected row signatures in any fixed row order i_1,...,i_A. By entropy chain rule, conditioning on the FULL earlier permutation rows instead of only earlier projected signatures can only lower each conditional entropy. At stage j, M=A-j+1 columns remain. Conditioned on prior full rows, Pi(i_j) uniform on remaining columns. If s of those columns lie in the retained positions of row i_j, the projected row signature has s distinct unit-vector outcomes each probability 1/M, plus the all-zero outcome of probability (M-s)/M. Its entropy is at least (s/M)log2 M. Under uniform Pi, E[s]=d_i M/A, so
    H(W) >= sum_j (d_(i_j)/A) log2(A-j+1).
Average this bound over all row orders (or choose an order no worse than average). Since each d_i appears uniformly at each position,
    log2 M(R) >= H(W) >= (r/A^2) log2(A!).
Thus at q=0
    r <= k A^2/log2(A!) = O(k A/log A).
The same applies separately to retained raw Y bits. Hence if c=A^2+o(A log A), only o(A^2) raw bits from each factor may bypass the prefix. For exact minimum width k=0 none can bypass. If all A^2 raw bits of one factor are retained, the bound is exact for its permutation signature: k>=ceil(log2(A!)). This is a necessary slack bound, not an assertion that this much slack suffices to retain the whole factor. With early ingress, replace k by c+q-N for the weaker entropy consequence, and use Section 2 for the sharper integer constraint. No need to count all possible permutations computationally for the analytic bound; can use a factorial log or matching certificate.

## 4. Conditional factor entropy improves selected patterns

A different bound holds without Z ingress, using uniform random product P (all its N entries independent fair bits) and independent uniform permutation Pi. Take Y=Pi^{-1}, X=P Pi; this permutes columns of P to get X and indeed XY=P. Let W be the retained raw X projection onto R. Any correct frontier code E determines both P and W. Therefore
    c >= H(E) >= H(P,W)=N+H(W|P),
so k>=H(W|P). This uses a different joint input distribution from Section 3; maximize the two valid slack lower bounds, not sum them. It can be stronger when many retained columns contain at least one position. For raw Y use row permutations symmetrically.

For column j of R let d_j be its retained row count. Process projected X columns in any order. Conditional on P and all earlier full permutation choices, the next column comes from M remaining source columns, uniformly chosen. Its retained d_j-bit pattern has the empirical distribution of those M patterns. Over random P and the independent permutation choices these patterns are iid uniform on 2^d_j symbols. Shannon entropy of any distribution is at least its collision entropy -log2(sum p_i^2), by concavity of log (or Jensen). The expected empirical collision probability is exactly 1/M+(1-1/M)2^(-d_j): two independent draws of a sample index coincide with probability 1/M, otherwise their independent labels coincide with probability 2^(-d_j). By Jensen on -log and conditioning on earlier full choices rather than earlier projected patterns,
    H(W|P) >= sum_j -log2(1/M_j + (1-1/M_j)2^(-d_j)), M_j=A-j+1,
where d_j follows the chosen column order. Can choose descending d_j for a valid bound (no optimal-order claim) or maximize over orders. For M=1 or d=0 the term is zero. E.g. a full retained X row has one retained coordinate in every column. More sharply, in that case for fixed P the signature is uniform among binary row vectors with its Hamming weight J. Thus
    H(W|P)=E log2 binom(A,J) = A-H(Binomial(A,1/2)) >= A-log2(A+1).
So a single whole raw row of X requires linear-in-A slack asymptotically, instead of only log2 A from its permutation signatures. A whole raw column of Y has the symmetric bound. This does NOT assert a whole X column or Y row has the same stronger bound. For small A this entropy bound can be weaker than Section 3; maximize. No claim of a new formula for conditional entropy under arbitrary early Z-code cells; uniform product may no longer be appropriate there.

## 5. Rigidity barrier with very small slack

The exact c=N,q=0 result says E=pi(P) for a bijection of the product cube, and monotone pi must be a coordinate permutation. With even logarithmic slack a literal-port stability conclusion is false, even for a monotone encoder and no Z ingress. For N a power of two >=4 (as in our A=2^b layout), define
    main_i = P_i AND OR_(j!=i) P_j, i=0,...,N-1;
    flag = OR_i P_i;
    label_l = OR_{i: bit_l(i)=1} P_i, l=0,...,log2 N-1.
There are N+1+log2 N ports. All are monotone in P, hence in X,Y. For product weight>=2 the main vector equals P. For weight zero or one it is all zero; flag distinguishes zero from a singleton and the binary label bits identify which singleton. Thus the decoder recovers P exactly with an arbitrary (nonmonotone allowed) map and then answers Z. No port function equals ANY P_i: each main needs a second product bit, each label ORs N/2>=2 entries, flag ORs all entries. All product masks attainable. This is an analytic counterexample to asserting most actual ports must be individual product outputs just because c=N+o(N) and encoder monotone. It does NOT avoid computing BMM cheaply: the construction may first compute all product outputs. It does not contradict the exact minimum-width theorem or a possible gate-cost stability theorem. A separate simple counterexample with one extra port is splitting one product entry into two disjoint groups of its inner-index witnesses and OR-decoding them, even with monotone decoder. The unresolved problem is the COST of near-width encoders, not the abstract ability to hide output functions.

## 6. Gate implications and limitations

If raw bypass count on each factor is o(N), then all but o(N) of the 2N independent factor bits must have a reader in the data-dependent prefix. This follows from topological frontier coverage and essentiality of triangle in every factor input; a primary not read inside the prefix must cross as a raw port. In fan-in-two this gives at least N-o(N) prefix gates by operand counting, still only linear. The new bounds also consume additional c+q budget in the exact prefix operand-slot ledger if specific raw bypasses are known. They are necessary topology tests even for nonmonotone circuits. They do NOT force a small SAT circuit to pass through a c=N+o(A log A), q=0 frontier, nor rule out a wide frontier of derived mixed signals, early Z mixing, or an arbitrary near-width nonmonotone code. Even at c=N the product-bijection encoding issue remains absent monotonicity. No numerical improvement on known unrestricted SAT circuit bounds. Do not add entropy bounds from different input distributions or different nested prefixes without a separate disjoint ledger. No new physical grammar or padding replay.

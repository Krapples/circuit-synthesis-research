## 1. Pivotal extension theorem

Let f=CLIQUE_(N,K), K>=3, with independently free edge bits. Fix a particular edge e={u,v}. Let S be a set of other edge variables and let alpha be ANY assignment to S. Write P for the graph consisting of exactly those edges of S fixed to 1. Let W consist of vertices incident to S other than u,v. Assume |W|<=N-K (a sufficient condition is 2|S|<=N-K).

Claim: there is an extension of alpha to all edges other than e under which e is pivotal (f with e=0 is 0, f with e=1 is 1) IFF P does not already contain a K-clique.

Necessity: a K-clique in the forced-one graph makes f=1 for both values of e under every extension.

Sufficiency: choose K-2 vertices outside W union {u,v}, possible by the premise. Let U be these vertices together with u,v. No edge of S is internal to U (e not in S), and no edge of S is incident to any of the K-2 chosen new vertices. Set all edges of U except e to 1, preserve alpha on S, and set every other edge to 0. When e=1 the induced U is a clique. When e=0 any K-clique containing a new vertex must lie wholly in U, because that vertex has no neighbors outside U, but U is missing e. Any K-clique containing none of the new vertices must use only edges in P, because the only potential edge of U among u,v is e=0. Neither is possible. This proves the claim for every assignment alpha, not merely an average or positive-density set.

Let b=binom(K,2), assume N>=K+2b (slightly stronger than needed). Then every partial assignment of fewer than b edges other than e extends to a pivotal context, since its forced-one graph has fewer than b edges and the vertex-footprint premise holds. The minimum size of a partial assignment on other edges making e irrelevant for ALL completions is therefore EXACTLY b: pin all b edges of a K-clique not containing e to 1 for a matching upper example. At exactly b assigned edges, irrelevance occurs iff those edges themselves form a complete K-clique and all are set to 1 (under the same N>=K+2b premise). For the lower threshold alone N>=K+2(b-1) suffices. This is a combinatorial certificate theorem, not a statement that the Boolean derivative is nonzero at every full assignment.

## 2. Consequence for actual sole readers

Work in the live simplified signed AIG after the hard-mode CLIQUE projection: binary AND gates, complemented wires and constants free. Remove constants, gates with identical/complementary operands and dead vertices. We may also remove any gate whose function is constant or a signed literal of another earlier vertex; the minimal-size realization certainly admits such normalization. For statements about a given nonminimal DAG, either require its relevant side signal actually takes both values, or perform this certified semantic simplification first. No finite-sample equality is sufficient. Syntactic support is the set of free input ancestors; semantic support is the set of essential inputs of the signal, possibly smaller.

Suppose e has exactly one distinct direct reader gate g. Its other operand h (signed wire, not another occurrence of e) cannot depend on e: any path from e into h would pass g and cause a cycle. Since g is AND, the side operand has a disabling value (signed h=0) that makes g independent of e. If h is constant in its enabling phase, g is a unary alias and can be removed; if h is constant disabling, e is inessential, impossible. In a normalized circuit h is nonconstant. For ANY assignment of the semantic support S of h attaining its disabling value, fixing that S assignment makes f independent of e for all remaining inputs, since every path from e goes through the disabled g.

Under N>=K+2(b-1), Section 1 shows that |S|>=b. Thus every sole-read edge must have a side operand with at least b essential other edge inputs, in particular at least b syntactic ancestors. Any such side gate cone contains at least b-1 binary gates by ordinary fan-in-two support counting.

Stronger description when the footprint of S outside the endpoints of e is <=N-K: every disabling assignment of h must itself force a K-clique among its known-one edges. Therefore on its support the disabling indicator is pointwise <=CLIQUE on the graph using only those S edges. In the minimum support case |S|=b and N>=K+2b, h can be nonconstant only if S is precisely the edge set of a K-clique not containing e. The signed enabling operand is then exactly NOT(AND of those b edge bits): the only possible disabling assignment is all ones, and nonconstancy requires it actually disable there. This is a classification of the minimal-support side signal, not of the rest of the circuit.

For a general proper B2 basis the same argument applies to a sole canalizing reader (all eight nonparity proper functions are canalizing in the side operand). XOR/XNOR readers have no disabling side value, so the read-multiplicity conclusion below is NOT asserted for arbitrary B2. The distinction matters; our previous lookup proofs counted XOR as a proper binary gate.

## 3. Hereditary population of multiple-read edges

Let L=K+2(b-1), with K>=3 and N>=L. Consider any normalized signed AIG computing Clique_(N,K), and any L-vertex set U. Fix all edges outside the complete graph on U to zero, leaving Clique_(L,K). Simplify constants and unary aliases and prune. If necessary perform the semantic removal of constant side signals; there is no requirement to implement this as an efficient production pass. All surviving gate IDs are original, and alias contraction cannot introduce new ancestry. Important distinction: alias contraction CAN increase the number of direct readers of a primary (one old reader could become an alias feeding many surviving gates). Therefore a multiple-read statement about the restricted simplified circuit cannot naively be transferred to original direct reader counts.

First, a result for the restricted circuit itself: choose the earliest gate in topological order whose SYNTACTIC data support has at least b variables. Such a gate exists since the output depends on all binom(L,2) edges and b>=3. Every input in its ancestor support must have at least two distinct direct readers IN THE SIMPLIFIED CIRCUIT. Otherwise its sole reader lies in this gate's ancestor cone or is the gate itself, and by Section 2 its other operand already has at least b syntactic ancestors, contradicting earliest choice. Thus at least b different free edge inputs are multiple-read in every such restricted circuit. In particular at the original full hard slice N>=L, before further restrictions, the same argument gives >=b multiple-read primaries. If D=binom(N,2) and B is the number of live proper binary gates, count operand slots: at least D+b direct primary occurrences, at least B-1 internal occurrences, hence B>=D+b-1. This is a small genuine numerical improvement over essential support D-1 for this signed-AIG slice only. It remains linear.

**Restriction-stable corrected transfer.** The appropriate original invariant is not direct reader multiplicity but branching reachable through a chain of unique readers. For each free edge primary e in the original hard slice, follow its unique successor while the current vertex has exactly one distinct live successor, stopping at the output or at a vertex of outdegree>=2. Call the latter event a branch-reachable edge. If a primary is not branch-reachable, its entire path to output consists of unique successors. Any restriction, constant/alias simplification and live pruning that preserves e essential leaves its surviving dependency paths a subgraph of contractions of this original path. It cannot make e have two distinct direct readers, since every original directed path from e to output was that unique path. A more general semantic alias rewrite could point to a functionally equal unrelated vertex, so for this transfer allow ONLY deletion of semantically constant gates and ordinary structural constant/unary alias contraction along existing operands, not replacement by unrelated equal functions. Removing a semantically constant side operand can be done by replacing it with its constant and propagating; this introduces no new path. That suffices for the Section 2 normalization premise.

Consequently every L-vertex induced set U contains at least b edge primaries that are branch-reachable IN THE ORIGINAL hard slice. Let R be their total number. Double count pairs (U,e), e branch-reachable and both endpoints in U:
    R * binom(N-2,L-2) >= b * binom(N,L).
Thus
    R >= ceil( b * binom(N,2) / binom(L,2) ).
For K=ceil(log2 N), L=Theta(K^2), this is Omega(N^2/(log N)^2). This is a hereditary necessary branching condition, stronger in exponent of log than the earlier polarity-conflicted input count numerically, but refers to a DIFFERENT property. A branch-reachable input need not itself be read twice or have both path phases. Do not claim Omega(N^2/log^2 N) original direct readers or original binate inputs. A single shared branching gate may account for many such primaries, so no corresponding additive distinct-gate charge follows from this count alone.

There is an additional elementary relation: any edge with no original reachable branch has one output path (ignoring parallel occurrence, removed in proper normalization). Such an input has only one phase along that path; monotonicity and essentiality of clique imply this phase positive. The converse need not hold: branching may preserve phase. The hereditary result concerns original hard-mode topology after fixing metadata, not the full programmable circuit in unit mode.

## 4. Negative control for charging large support cones separately

For any vertex subset V0 of size K, let h be the indicator that all b edges within V0 exist. Compute h once with b-1 ANDs. For every edge outside that subset of b edges, form y_e=x_e AND NOT h. Keep inside edges unchanged, and output h OR Clique_(N,K)(y), using any circuit for Clique as the remaining core. If h=1, output true; if h=0, y=x, so this is a correct circuit. Every outside raw edge is read just once in its own gating AND (the rest reads y_e). Their shared side operand has exactly b essential bits and realizes the minimal-support NAND classification. The construction adds D-b masking gates and one shared h cone, plus the final OR, to a circuit for Clique; it does not give a small circuit for Clique itself. This shows why the b-1 side-cone bound cannot be charged separately to every sole-read edge: the same actual side gate can serve all of them. It also illustrates that a local large-support requirement by itself cannot imply a superlinear bound. No inference of a universal size lower bound from this upper transformation.

## 6. Next direction

The scarce resource remains distinct original branching gates, not the number of inputs that can reach them. The new precise local obstruction identifies which side signals can disable a sole-read input and what their minimal support must be. To go further one needs to exploit simultaneous restrictions to incompatible vertex sets and prove that too much reuse of the same branch/side signal destroys necessary edge sensitivity, or find a stronger nonmonotonicity resource that survives alias contraction. The explicit shared NAND control rules out simply summing large cones. No new unrestricted superlinear lower bound, no production integration, independent audit/Lean outstanding. L-clb-036 remains open.

# Pair-cofactor incompatibility forces distinct first branches in signed AIGs

Date: 2026-11-26. Base c2ffa513b0129c67fee38e7556f4711b05a2405e. Extends [CLIQUE pivotal support](clique_pivotal_support_handoff.md), [switchable incidence](switchable_incidence_handoff.md), and [programmable tag residuals](programmable_tag_residual_handoff.md). New general signed-AIG topology theorem, pairwise cofactor completeness of the switchable physical layout, and a stronger unconditional ORIGINAL payload-ancestry bound by exact operand-slot accounting. This is a paper result and finite replay, not Lean verification, an independent proof audit, a production change or an unrestricted superlinear SAT bound. L-clb-036 remains open.

## 1. General first-branch separation theorem

Model: live signed AIG, AND gates of fan-in two, complemented wires/NOT and constants free. Each gate has two distinct nonconstant underlying operands after ordinary constant/unary/duplicate-input simplification. Work with one output; remove dead gates. These simplifications can only remove original IDs or contract paths along existing edges. Do not perform unrelated functional rewrites for a topology-specific statement. An essential designated data primary reaches the output. Starting at such a primary, follow its unique distinct live successor for as long as there is exactly one, and stop at the first vertex having two or more distinct successors, or at the output. Call this endpoint beta(x); it can be the primary x itself, a gate, or the output. If there are >=2 essential primaries, the output is not a raw primary.

**Lemma.** If two distinct essential inputs x,y have the same endpoint beta, then there is a fixed two-input signed AND function h of x,y such that EVERY cofactor on x,y in which BOTH inputs remain essential is h or its complement. All other primaries, including all metadata/query inputs, may be fixed arbitrarily and differently for each cofactor.

Proof: the unique-successor paths from x,y to their common endpoint first meet at a gate g (a raw primary cannot be the common endpoint of two paths). Up to g the arms are disjoint and each vertex prior to g has exactly one distinct successor. Every path from either primary to output passes g; before g there is no branching. At any gate along one arm strictly before g, its other operand has no ancestry from x or y: a path from either of them to that operand would have to leave one of the unique arms before g, intersect the two arms before their first meeting, or go back from g or a later descendant in violation of acyclicity. With other primaries fixed, such side operands are constants. If the cofactor depends on both x,y, no arm gate can be disabled (a constant there would make its primary irrelevant everywhere downstream). An AND with its other signed operand fixed to the enabling value 1 simply passes the signed arm operand. Hence the two values entering g are x and y with fixed phases determined by the corresponding edge signs, independent of which context keeps both essential. Thus g computes a fixed signed AND h. Everything downstream, when the other primaries are fixed and g is supplied externally, is a function of the one bit g alone: deleting g separates x,y from output. In a context where both are essential this unary function must be identity or NOT. The cofactor is h or NOT h.

In particular, if some fixing of the other inputs yields x AND y and some fixing yields x OR y, their endpoints cannot be equal. The two functions depend on both inputs and are neither equal nor complementary. This proof needs fixed signs on the unique arms: arbitrary B2 parity gates can change arm polarity with their other operand. Concrete negative control in general B2:
    F(x,y,q) = ((x XOR q) AND (y XOR q)) XOR q.
At q=0 this is AND, at q=1 OR; both data inputs have the same endpoint (the output), and there is no branching among data-dependent gates or data primaries. This is why the new theorem is about signed AIG/De Morgan primitives, not arbitrary binary gates or the free-affine XOR-AND gate model.

## 2. Exact slot identity combines branch and residual obligations

Let S consist of d independently free essential designated data primaries; other inputs are query or fixed. Let B count live AND gate IDs with syntactic data ancestry. Assume the output is a counted gate (true in our applications). Every successor of a counted gate is counted. Define:
- I = total operand occurrences directly from S;
- E = total operand occurrences between counted gates;
- Q_D = total operand occurrences in counted gates from data-free sources (query-only wires; constants may be removed);
- e_data=I-d, e_internal=E-(B-1).
Both excesses are nonnegative, since every essential primary is read at least once and every nonoutput counted gate has at least one outgoing edge in the live cone. Exact count of two operand slots at every gate gives
    B=d-1+Q_D+e_data+e_internal.
Each distinct branching vertex in S contributes at least one to e_data; each counted branching gate contributes at least one to e_internal. A first-branch endpoint of a designated primary is of one of these two types, except the output endpoint, which contributes zero.

Form an incompatibility graph G on S. Join two data primaries whenever their two-variable cofactors (fixing every other primary, in possibly different allowed contexts) include two functions each essential in both variables and not equal or complementary. The weaker sufficient test of an AND and an OR cofactor also works. By Section 1, assigning beta(x) to each x is a proper coloring of G. Hence at least chi(G) endpoints, of which at most one is the output; all other endpoints are distinct branching vertices. Thus
    e_data+e_internal >= chi(G)-1.
If N distinct residual functions of the whole data cube occur as the query varies, their outputs must be determined by the Q_D query-only operand values and the data, so N<=2^Q_D. This assumes the legal query domain is independent of the full data cube for the residual count. The pair incompatibility witnesses themselves need only be legal two-variable subcubes; the same single fixed circuit must be correct on them. Combine the two constraints through the exact slot identity, NOT by adding separate gate-complexity bounds:
    B >= d-2+chi(G)+ceil(log2 N).
If every pair is incompatible, chi(G)=d and
    B >= 2d-2+ceil(log2 N).
For a fixed topology, retain the stronger statement that each first-branch basin is an independent set of G (and the actual Q_D occurrences must meet the residual bound). No requirement that all data paths from different endpoints are disjoint later. Each branch vertex is charged just once to the appropriate excess in the one exact operand ledger. This does not prove any superlinear charge; at most d distinct endpoints are needed.

One can simplify the original SAT circuit after a restriction fixing nondata primaries, then apply the theorem: every remaining gate is an original ID, and alias contraction cannot create a new original data ancestor. Direct-reader counts can increase after contraction, so we do NOT transfer a direct-reader count backward. Instead for witnesses already valid in the original graph the first-branch lemma applies directly, or transfer the number of surviving counted gate IDs. Signed phases in aliases are incorporated in the resulting AIG.

## 3. CLIQUE has complete pair incompatibility

For K>=4, N>=2K, every pair of distinct edge primaries e,f admits AND and OR cofactors in Clique_(N,K):
- AND: choose K vertices containing the endpoints of both edges, set all edges of that complete graph except e,f to one and every other edge to zero. A K-clique exists exactly when both e,f exist.
- OR: choose two K-vertex sets, one containing endpoints of e and one endpoints of f, with intersection empty for disjoint e,f, or exactly their common endpoint for incident e,f. Set union of their complete-graph edges except e,f to one and all others zero. Every K-clique is confined to one of these two sets (no edge joins their exclusive parts; K>=3). The verdict is e OR f.
All conditions are on ordinary edge assignments; no promise ambiguity. In the previously committed positive single-copy physical CLIQUE restriction, this proves B>=2 binom(N,2)-2 for a normalized signed AIG. Moreover at most ONE edge primary has a completely unbranched path to the output, and the others have mutually DISTINCT first branching vertices, some of which may themselves be primaries. This strengthens the earlier hereditary branch-reachable population result for these parameter ranges. It is not a claim that nearly every edge is directly read twice, nor a new asymptotic exponent. The word length is Theta(N^2 log N), so this numerical slice bound is weaker than existing full-SAT bounds at those lengths. No external monotone lower-bound theorem needed here.

## 4. Every pair of physical payload coordinates in switchable incidence is incompatible

Reuse switchable_incidence_handoff.md without modifying the layout: t=3m programmable literal slots grouped in triples, ell payload bits per slot, D=t ell; mode p=1 yields unit conjunction, p=0 yields genuine existential triple disjunction. Tag 0 is the forced arbitrary dictionary h, tag 1 complementary witness pairs, tags >=2 ordinary unforced variable namespaces. Each physical payload bit occurs once as a positive low digit. The following pair witnesses use only dictionary and tags>=2; the independent tag-1 complement clauses are harmless.

Let x,y be ANY two different designated payload bits. Fix every other payload bit to zero. All metadata in each of the two contexts below are constants chosen independently of x,y. All unused slots get distinct fresh tags, positive signs, so their unit constraints or disjunctions can be satisfied independently. In particular no unused slot shares the helper namespace used below.

Case A: bits in the SAME slot. Use unit mode, that slot tag 0 with positive sign. Program h(p) equal to AND of the two selected pattern coordinates for the first context and OR for the second. All other slots inactive. Exactly the desired cofactors, including for arbitrary values of any irrelevant fixed payload bits (here zero).

Case B: DIFFERENT slots. Program h(p)=1 iff p!=0. Since each selected slot has all other pattern bits zero, its positive dictionary literal equals its free physical bit, even if the two bits are at different coordinate positions. AND cofactor: unit mode, both selected slots tag 0, all others inactive. OR cofactor:
- If both selected slots are in the same triple, switch to existential mode and make its third literal the false dictionary entry (tag0, pattern0, positive). Its clause is x OR y OR false. Other triples inactive.
- If selected slots belong to distinct triples, switch to existential mode. Each triple has two other slots available. In the first use the same fresh unforced variable z (tag2,pattern0,positive) and the false dictionary literal. In the second use NOT z (same tag2,pattern0,negative) and the false dictionary literal. Thus the two clauses are (x OR z) and (y OR NOT z). Existence of z is equivalent to x OR y: if both bits zero the clauses conflict; if x=1 choose z=0; if y=1 choose z=1. All remaining slots use mutually distinct tags >=3. There are enough tags in the original reserved namespace (t+2 tags including 0,1). No extra physical clauses, no copies of the two free bits, no change of length or positions.
The metadata switch and helper literals are different between AND and OR contexts, but that is allowed because the same original SAT circuit is correct on both constant restrictions.

This also works on EVERY nonempty data subset S after setting all other designated bits to zero, since a pair witness fixes every other bit to zero anyway. Therefore for d=|S|>=2 the incompatibility graph is complete on S. This is a new cross-context structural obligation on the original physical coordinates. For d=1 no branch lower bound is needed; use chi=1.

## 5. Stronger simultaneous ancestry lower bound

Let A(S) be the set of original live AIG gate IDs descended from any designated bit in S. Let d=|S|>0, a be the number of occupied slots, and s_i the number of S bits in each occupied slot. Fix outside-S payloads to zero. The positive/negative singleton selection contexts from switchable_incidence_handoff.md show that every S bit is essential and the output is a counted gate. Keep the common legal metadata domain containing all pair contexts, graph contexts and one-dictionary-slot contexts. Every such metadata assignment gives a legal input for every S assignment, although a specific pair cofactor is only extracted after fixing the other S bits. The previous programmable unit-tag theorem applies in unit mode with tag>=2 in place of the old nonzero tag namespace, independently of the extra satisfiable witness clauses. Let a_j count labeled disjoint unions of complete bipartite components and isolates as in that handoff. A certified residual count is
    R(S) = max(a_a, max_i a_(a-1)*(2^(2^s_i)-1)).
The graph residual functions are pairwise distinct even on heterogeneous retained coordinate sets. The second count reserves one dictionary slot and combines any nonzero function there with an independently programmed graph on the other slots. Take MAX, not sum.

Section 2 and completeness of the incompatibility graph give the simultaneous necessary inequality
    |A(S)| >= 2d-2+ceil(log2 R(S)).
The restricted simplified circuit counts distinct remaining IDs, all of which were in A(S) originally; this transfers the result to the original full SAT DAG. No addition across different S. For a single occupied slot with s bits the dictionary bound gives |A(S)|>=2s-2+2^s. For s=1 this is 2, consistent with the phase-complete selector contexts. For full payload S, take a=t=3m, D=3m ell:
    |A(S)| >= 2D-2+ceil(log2 a_(3m)).
Compare the prior operand-only bound D-1+ceil(log2 a_(3m)) on this SAME layout. The improvement is D-1 gates in the numerical necessary budget, and the added charge is justified by distinct first endpoints via the exact slot count.

For m=2^k, ell=floor(k/4), log2 a_(3m)=(1-o(1))3mk by the prior labeled graph count. Thus the lower bound is (6-o(1))D, versus (5-o(1))D previously. The switchable word length n=(37.5+o(1))mk and D=(.75+o(1))mk, so this equals at least (0.12-o(1))n on the unpadded lengths. For ell=k, log2 a_(3m)=(1-o(1))D, giving (3-o(1))D, versus (2-o(1))D previously, or (1/8-o(1))n since n=(72+o(1))mk and D=3mk. These are lower bounds on designated payload ANCESTRY, not total SAT lower bounds that can be added to other input charges. They do not exceed the best repository-wide general SAT size lower bounds. All asymptotic length/padding facts reused; no new padding verification.

## 6. Core reserve variant

If a chosen retained core consists of the S bits in a collection of complete triple GROUPS, with d_J retained bits, all its pair cofactor witnesses can use helper slots inside those same triples, never an erased outside group. Keep these core metadata contexts and the unit graph/dictionary contexts on its occupied slots while deleting S bits outside the chosen groups by the prior two-gate elimination rule. Outside groups are inactive in all core contexts, so even arbitrary deleted bit values are irrelevant. Before deletion each erased bit has its own singleton-selector context; afterward some retained bit remains essential. Signed AIG sole-reader deletion needs only canalizing constant substitution (no XOR macro). After all outside deletions, the remaining circuit has the complete pair incompatibility and certified residual count R_J on the core. If the core is nonempty, charge disjoint original deleted and surviving IDs:
    |A(S)| >= 2(d-d_J)+(2d_J-2+ceil(log2 R_J))
            = 2d-2+ceil(log2 R_J).
This is at most the full residual bound if using its same graph/dictionary subfamily, but shows the stronger branch structure survives a legitimate deletion sequence. For a core containing all payloads in its retained groups, the earlier fixed-metadata hard incidence context can also be retained; replace the surviving term by the MAX of the displayed branch/residual floor and a certified ordinary AIG size bound for H_(r,ell). Do not add these two lower bounds on the same surviving gates. Do not extrapolate the core transfer to arbitrary selected slots leaving helper slots in a partially erased triple; those helpers might depend on erased bits. No superlinear hard-core bound is provided.

## 8. Limitations and next step

The earlier issue was that many branch-reachable inputs could share one branching vertex. The first-branch incompatibility theorem fixes that for this cofactor-complete family: first branching vertices must be distinct, and the extra fanout occurrences they require are disjoint in the operand ledger from query-only occurrences. This is a genuine stronger linear bound on the actual packed SAT graph, without relying on translation rank or a monotonicity assumption on the circuit. It applies to the signed AIG model, not unrestricted B2 gates at the same numerical cost; constant-factor simulation does not preserve the displayed coefficient. The bound remains linear, and the hard incidence family being NP-complete does not justify claiming a superlinear lower bound. Independent auditing and Lean formalization outstanding, no production changes.

The next research challenge is to force more than one distinct branch resource per input, or constrain reuse of paths AFTER their first branch using higher-arity cofactor witnesses. Pairwise incompatibility alone is capped by the number of data primaries; a graph coloring bound cannot force more than d first endpoints. A hypothetical hierarchy must account for reconvergence and avoid charging the same gate or fanout occurrence at multiple stages. The shared side-function control of clique_pivotal_support_handoff.md remains a valid warning. L-clb-036 remains open.

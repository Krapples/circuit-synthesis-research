# Cubic witnesses: counting obstruction and deterministic component compression

Previous quadratic deterministic compression: [deterministic_quadratic_handoff.md](deterministic_quadratic_handoff.md), whose sparse circuit-cost argument still requires independent audit. This round proves a counting obstruction and a new deterministic quasi-linear upper bound for a specified cubic subpromise. It does not establish an upper or lower bound for unrestricted SAT. L-clb-036 remains open.

## 1. Cubic packed projection (paper construction)

Use M=2^k, ell, J, m=kJ, A and the dictionary/guard layout of [quadratic_compression_handoff.md](quadratic_compression_handoff.md). Give each physical slot three programmable labels u,v,w in {0,...,m}. The same common-width pool of A+3m+1 auxiliary IDs suffices. In the quadratic block replace the three AND clauses by four clauses defining p=e_u AND e_v AND e_w:

    (-p OR e_u), (-p OR e_v), (-p OR e_w),
    (p OR -e_u OR -e_v OR -e_w).

The equality clauses t=U_pattern and four XOR clauses z=t XOR p are unchanged. The common identifier width makes the new block length constant. The pattern digits still occur exactly twice in fixed positions; the table digits occur once, all disjoint, for every permutation and label triple. Set e_0=0 as before. The word is Theta(M) bits; padding and signed balancing transfer. Repeated labels permit degree-one and degree-two monomials by Boolean multilinearization; a zero label suppresses the contribution. Constants come from the selector parity offset b. For fixed witnesses all other values are forced before the output assertion, so exact SAT is whether h intersects the image of F=b XOR Q, a degree-at-most-three map GF(2)^q -> GF(2)^k. The earlier delta-free high-path theorem persists by pinning all labels to zero. This is an exact paper construction; the actual cubic paired grammar has NOT been executed in this round.

The minimum-weight argument from the previous handoff still gives every nonempty address fiber density >=2^(-3k) and an accepting witness of weight <=3k. Random affine dimension 3k+2 suffices for decision with one-sided error <=1/4; 4k+2 preserves the whole image with probability >=3/4. The proof is exactly the previous pairwise-independent affine sampling argument. However exact cubic character sums do not admit the previous quadratic pair-elimination identity as a general efficient algorithm.

## 2. Sharp distinction: cubic character sums encode #3SAT

Let a 3-CNF have c clauses, and let a_i,b_i,d_i be the three literal-falsity bits of clause i (each an affine polynomial of its input variable). Introduce z_i and enforce two quadratic equations per clause:

    g_(2i) = z_i + a_i b_i = 0,
    g_(2i+1) = z_i d_i = 0.

Each satisfying assignment of the original variables extends uniquely to the z_i. Conversely any solution of the quadratic equations satisfies every original clause. Let y_0,...,y_(2c-1) be fresh variables and form the single degree-at-most-three scalar polynomial

    P(x,z,y) = sum_j y_j g_j(x,z)   over GF(2).

Summing over y first gives

    C(P) = sum_(x,z,y) (-1)^P = 2^(2c) #SAT(original formula).

Indeed, for any nonzero constraint vector g the signed sum over y is zero, while for g=0 it is 2^(2c). The monomial count, number of used variables and sparse-record representation are O(c + used original variables); coefficients are obtained by constant-size expansions per clause. This is a direct reduction from #3SAT. In particular exact cubic character sum computation on sparse polynomials is #P-hard, and testing whether this nonnegative character sum is positive on the constructed subclass is NP-hard. No assumption about #P vs FP is needed for the reduction; a polynomial-time exact general cubic sum algorithm would imply FP=#P.

The scalar polynomial can also be placed in row 0 of the packed cubic layout, with all other rows zero, by choosing k with J large enough for its O(c) terms and labels. Use repeated labels for degree below 3; the multiplier y makes a constant term unnecessary. Choose the natural scale with M=O((c+1) log^2(c+2)) for sufficiently large c (J=Theta(M/k^2), bounded adjacent-scale ratio). Therefore exact witness histogram recovery is #P-hard even for an appropriate one-active-row packed cubic family, after the trivial factor conversion C(P)=N(0)-N(1). This does NOT establish NP-hardness of image membership for that family: a nonconstant scalar Boolean polynomial has image {0,1}, regardless of its character sum. In particular the SAT-decision promise in this reduction is not a reduction from 3SAT. Do not use counting hardness as a gate-count lower bound or claim that computing the exact compressed histogram on O(k) variables is #P-hard relative to the full table size M.

The general bias-versus-rank literature on cubics is relevant to future work; see Haramaty and Shpilka, On the Structure of Cubic and Quartic Polynomials, ECCC TR09-080, https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2009/080/. The counting reduction and component theorem below are proved directly, without importing a quantitative cubic regularity result or claiming literature priority.

## 3. Exact image compression for independent degree-bounded components

Let F(x)=b XOR sum_i F_i(x_i) in GF(2)^k, where the variable sets x_i are disjoint and each F_i has degree <=3. Move constants to b, so F_i(0)=0. A component need not be a single monomial or a quadratic map. Represent each nonconstant monomial by its k-bit coefficient label. Let V_i be the linear span of these labels. This is also the linear span of F_i's image: every output is a sum of coefficient labels, and each monomial coefficient is an XOR of evaluations by Boolean Mobius inversion. We use the coefficient representation to avoid enumerating any component during selection.

Algorithm: initially all components are residual. In each of R=8k rounds, scan all residual nonconstant coefficient records and greedily build a basis of their span. Select every component supplying a basis vector (at most k components); remove selected components from the residual set. Stop early if the span is zero. Let S denote the union of selected components, V the span of the final residual coefficient labels, and Lambda=V^perp of dimension t. Then at most 8k^2 components are selected and

    image(sum_(i in S) F_i) = image(sum_i F_i).

The same equality holds with b added. Proof:

- For every lambda outside Lambda, at least one final residual label has nonzero dot product with lambda. In every completed round, the basis of that round's residual labels spans this label, so at least one selected basis vector has nonzero dot product with lambda. Its component is distinct across rounds. Early termination with span zero cannot occur when lambda outside Lambda exists. Hence at least 8k selected independent components have nonconstant scalar polynomial lambda dot F_i.
- Any nonconstant degree-at-most-three scalar polynomial and its complement have density >=1/8, by the minimum-weight lemma. Its signed bias therefore has absolute value <=3/4. All other component biases have magnitude <=1. Independence multiplies biases: for every lambda outside Lambda, the selected sum has absolute bias <=(3/4)^(8k); adding residual components cannot increase this bound.
- For lambda in Lambda every residual scalar polynomial is identically zero. Thus the marginal map of the selected sum onto the t coordinates of a basis of Lambda has exactly the same distribution as the corresponding full marginal. Fix an address a whose marginal is attained. Its marginal density p is >=2^(-3t), since the fiber indicator is a nonzero degree-at-most-3t polynomial. By Fourier inversion, the sum of the signed character terms over Lambda equals 2^t p>=2^(-2t)>=2^(-2k).
- The total absolute contribution outside Lambda is at most 2^k (3/4)^(8k)<2^(-2k), since (3/4)^8<1/8. Thus Fourier inversion gives positive probability for a under the selected sum. Conversely if the marginal is absent, the address is absent under both maps. The image of either map is exactly the inverse image of the same marginal support. This proves equality. The selected image is of course also contained in the full image by setting residual variables to zero.

For a fixed general degree D>=2, use bias bound 1-2^(1-D) and R sufficiently large that (1-2^(1-D))^R<2^(-Dk); R=O_D(k) suffices, giving O_D(k^2) components. The proof applies to arbitrary component dimensions; the efficient circuit conclusion below additionally bounds their size. For D=1 a single span selection suffices. The compression is input-dependent, so it does not conflict with the previous oblivious-sketch counterexample.

## 4. Deterministic quasi-linear circuits for variable-incidence small components

Promise: the raw witness-interaction hypergraph (after removing any monomial containing the pinned zero label and deduplicating repeated variables within a monomial, but before cancellation between monomials) has components of at most k witness variables. The vertices are the m nonzero label IDs; a monomial connects all of its distinct variables. The promise is sufficient, not necessary. The labels, component partition, row permutation, pattern bits and table may all vary from instance to instance. On this promise the entire packed cubic family has deterministic O(n log^C n) signed binary AIG circuits for some absolute C. This does not require computing all 2^q witness assignments.

Here is the bounded-fan-in implementation, using the standard sorting/segmented-scan approach from the earlier handoffs.

1. Extract the O(m) slot records (three O(k)-bit label IDs, logical row ID, pattern parity), eliminate zero-containing witness terms, and deduplicate repeated variables in each remaining term. Use comparisons with the k fixed row intervals to obtain k-bit one-hot row coefficient labels. Separately XOR the pattern parities into b. The raw hypergraph becomes an O(m)-edge multigraph by including all pairs in each at-most-three-variable term. Keep isolated vertices as needed.
2. Discover component IDs by k rounds of synchronous minimum-label propagation along these edges. In each round, route both endpoint queries to the current vertex labels by sorting and segmented copy, and reduce neighbor labels by segmented minimum, retaining the old own label. A connected component of <=k vertices has diameter <=k-1, so its final label is its minimum vertex. Record lengths O(k), O(m) records, sorting O(m log^2 m) compare-exchanges with O(k) gates each, and k rounds give O(n polylog n) gates. This uses no unproved general connectivity circuit bound. The connectedness promise is on raw edges, so cancellation cannot invalidate the decomposition.
3. Route monomial records to their component ID. Assign each vertex a local index by sorting vertices on (component ID, vertex ID) and scanning with a segment counter. Canonicalize each monomial's local indices, sort by (component, local monomial), and XOR the k-bit coefficient labels in each run. This cancellation is essential: the span must use actual nonzero coefficients, not raw monomial occurrences. Compact or mark segment ends. A component of <=k variables now has at most sum_(j=1..3) binom(k,j)=O(k^3) nonconstant monomial records.
4. Implement the component selection with R=8k passes over a fixed record array. Each pass builds a k-bit echelon basis with a sequential unrolled scan, O(k^2) gates per vector using masked pivot updates. Store the component ID for each newly inserted pivot. Mark all records from these <=k chosen components inactive using comparisons to the pivot tags (or sorting). Keep chosen IDs across rounds, padded to 8k^2 slots with a validity bit. Early stopping is unnecessary: empty passes produce no selections. This costs O(m poly(k)) gates and O(k^2) selected component slots.
5. Gather the coefficient records of each chosen component into its own fixed O(k^3)-capacity array using sorting/segmented routing, or by scanning all records separately for each of the O(k^2) chosen IDs with conditional writes indexed by local monomial. Either implementation is O(m poly(k,log m)) gates. A simpler fixed-size gather loops over chosen IDs and all O(k^3) local monomial keys, masking and XOR-reducing matching records; this is explicitly O(m poly(k)) and avoids dynamic memory. Pad to k local variables. Evaluate each selected component on all 2^k possible assignments to that padded block, using O(k^4) gates per evaluation. Collect the attained addresses into a length-M Boolean indicator by sorting the M output keys together with the M address keys and segmented OR/copy; O(M poly(k)) gates per selected component. Inactive slots have image {0}.
6. Starting with {0}, combine these O(k^2) indicators by exact XOR sumset convolution. For Boolean indicators A,B, the integer convolution C(a)=sum_x A(x)B(a XOR x) is recovered by two forward and one inverse Walsh-Hadamard transforms, pointwise integer multiplication, and division by M. The transforms take O(M k) additions of O(k)-bit signed integers; pointwise products use O(k^2) gates each. In the inverse transform intermediate magnitudes are at most M^3, so O(k) bits suffice throughout. Threshold C(a)>0 immediately after each convolution. This avoids exponential growth of witness-count integers. Finally test whether the shifted indicator intersects h: use a k-stage XOR-index permutation network to shift by b, then AND and OR with h. Total O(M poly(k)) gates.

With M=Theta(n), k=Theta(log n), m=O(M/k), all fixed arrays and operations above have near-linear size up to fixed powers of log n. Signed AIG conversion changes size by a constant factor. Thus the small-component variable-incidence cubic promise cannot serve as a standalone lower-bound witness for O(n^1.01). This is an analytical circuit-cost result, not gate synthesis. It is independent of the earlier deterministic quadratic circuit theorem, since the only exact local image computation is exhaustive on <=k variables.

Corollary: if a set of s witness labels is supplied in the promise (or fixed in advance), and deleting them leaves raw components of <=k variables, enumerate its 2^s assignments. Each resulting polynomial still has degree <=3, and its remaining interaction graph lies within the graph after deletion. Run the same circuit per assignment and OR. Size O(2^s n log^C n). Therefore s=O(log log n) retains quasi-linear size; even s<=epsilon log_2 n with fixed epsilon<0.01 yields o(n^1.01). Finding such a separator without it being supplied is not claimed. Large connected cubic cores are not covered.

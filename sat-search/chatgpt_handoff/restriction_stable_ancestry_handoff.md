# Restriction-stable ancestry pruning and equality-budget selector cascade rigidity

Date: 2026-11-26. Starts at c14a023747052bd44e5692df04e4d5f58aa643a7; continues [binary selector elimination](binary_selector_elimination_handoff.md). Rank-free B2 structural results. New phase-independent topology rejection rule, an unbounded deficit family relative to the old ancestry matching relaxation, and necessary reader/branch structure at the previous lower-bound budget. No new unconditional numerical SAT size lower bound, independent audit, Lean verification, production modification, new physical grammar, or originality claim. L-clb-036 remains open.

## 1. Model and optimistic restriction quotient

As in the prior binary-selector handoff: ordinary proper two-input Boolean gates, all ten truth tables allowed, signed wires/NOT, constants and fanout free; query-only preprocessing arbitrary and uncharged. Data-dependent count means syntactic data ancestry, not semantic dependence. Work in the live output cone. Proper gates have two different nonconstant operand vertices after removing trivial signed aliases; when a specific gate assignment is considered, its unary/constant restrictions can be simplified exactly. This is not the XOR-AND model.

For topology-only pruning with unspecified truth tables, fix a subset S of original data primaries to arbitrary constants. Form the **optimistic quotient** O_S(C) as follows. Track an unsigned representative of each original vertex in topological order, with 0 denoting an unspecified constant. Map fixed primaries and existing constants to 0, other primaries to themselves. For each binary gate, look up the two operand representatives (ignore phases):
- two zero operands: represent by 0;
- exactly one zero: represent by the other operand;
- two equal nonzero representatives: represent by that representative;
- two distinct nonzero representatives: retain original gate ID with those representatives as operands.
Finally retain only the cone of the optimistic representative of the original output. No semantic assertions are made that the actual output agrees with these aliases. In particular a unary gate might actually be constant; optimism keeps its maximum possible ancestry. Unspecified constant values can be merged into 0 since any combination of constants is constant, regardless of their values. Treat query-only primary signals as distinct even when retained queries impose relations or constants; ignoring those further restrictions is safe.

**Containment lemma.** For every assignment to S and every choice of proper binary truth tables/phases, the actual signed constant/alias simplification of the original circuit retains a subset of the gate IDs of O_S(C). For each retained ID its actual surviving data ancestors are a subset of its optimistic data ancestors.

Proof without live pruning: let o(v) be the optimistic representative and a(v) the actual signed representative of each original vertex. By induction on the original topological order, if o(v)=0 then a(v) is constant. If o(v)=u nonzero, then a(v) is either constant or a signed version of the actual representative of u. When a gate is optimistically collapsed because an operand is zero or the two representatives agree, the actual operands are either constants or signed versions of a single actual representative, so its truth table becomes at most unary; its actual representative satisfies this assertion. When a gate is optimistically kept, the assertion for u=v is tautological. Thus no optimistically collapsed ID can survive in the actual graph. Furthermore any optimistically retained gate that actually becomes an alias points to a constant or an actual representative of one of its optimistic operands. Contracting these paths gives the actual dependencies and cannot introduce data ancestors. The actual output representative is either constant or a retained vertex in the optimistic output cone; recursively each live actual operand comes from an ancestor in that cone. This proves the claim after live pruning as well. It compares the two specified simplification passes, not arbitrary circuit resynthesis which could invent new IDs.

This pass is linear in the DAG size, before ancestry bitsets and matching. Cascades can occur when formerly different signals become equal after alias substitution: two gates reading x and the same y both become at most unary in y after x is fixed, so any gate reading those two gates also becomes at most unary. No particular truth table is required for this collapse.

## 2. Restriction-stable subset and matching theorem

Let the query target be a selector of D independent data bits, every bit available as a residual, with query-only decoding arbitrary. The earlier theorem gives B_D>=2D-1 for D>=2, and every subset T of data has |A_C(T)|>=2|T|-1 if T is the whole data set, >=2|T| otherwise. For D=1 the exact data-dependent count is zero, not one.

For EVERY S of erased data bits leaving a set R of at least two, restrict the allowed queries to those selecting only R, and fix S to ANY constants. The target on R is still a selector. Apply the earlier subset theorem to the actual simplified graph and then the containment lemma:
    |A_{O_S(C)}(T)| >= 2|T| - 1_{T=R}     for all T subset R.
A counts only live retained original proper binary gate IDs reachable from T (empty T gives zero). Equivalently, for EVERY choice of one exempt surviving bit, a bipartite matching must allocate one original surviving descendant gate to that bit and two to each other bit. This equivalence follows from Hall: necessity of all subset inequalities follows by choosing an exempt outside T when T is proper; sufficiency as in earlier handoff. Matchings at different restrictions need not be identical and cannot be added as independent charges. A single chosen exempt gives a cheaper sound necessary test; all choices are needed for the exact equivalence.

For the batched product target AND_j H_j[q_j], retain at least one entry in each group by deleting S and restricting its query domain. Let f(T) count remaining groups entirely contained in T (the groups here mean their surviving entries). If f(T)=0, the old deletion proof works unchanged by keeping a selectable entry outside T in every group and deleting all T, giving |A(T)|>=2|T|. If f(T)>=1, first fix all data outside T to 1. Partially included groups can be bypassed by selecting one such fixed-one entry. If at least one fully included group has >=2 entries, leave one such group with two entries and each other fully included group with one. The previous exact core lemma yields
    |A(T)|>=2|T|-f(T).
If all fully included groups are singleton, delete every optional T entry, leaving the AND of f(T) data bits. Its data-dependent B2 count is >=f(T)-1 by the ordinary fan-in-two live-cone leaf count, even with arbitrary free query signals. Hence
    |A(T)|>=2|T|-f(T)-1.
In these proofs each deleted bit is essential before removal, after removal another essential data bit remains; the original-ID two-gate charge is unchanged. This extends the old subset theorem to restricted groups including singleton cases. In particular if at least one remaining group has >=2 entries, whole-set bound is 2|R|-r; if all remaining groups singleton, it is r-1. Apply each of these to the optimistic quotient using containment. If every remaining group has >=2 entries, all the old per-group exemption matchings apply verbatim. Do not apply the stronger unqualified old Hall demand to singleton-only full subsets without the one-gate correction.

**Local slack corollary.** For a selector let s=2D-1+sigma. For any S leaving >=2 entries, at most 2|S|+sigma original data-dependent gates can disappear or become query-only in O_S(C), since the remaining data-dependent count must be >=2(D-|S|)-1. In particular at tight budget every erased primary can force at most two counted gate removals (including actual simplification, which is also a valid restricted circuit). The same local slack corollary holds for batched product with s=2D-r+sigma, whenever deletion leaves all groups nonempty and at least one nonsingleton group. If all groups become singleton, the allowed removal bound has an extra +1. If only the direct-reader count is known, erasing S removes at least all distinct direct readers of S; the quotient improves on that by counting alias collisions, cascades, dead cones and gates losing data ancestry. This yields practical incremental topology certificates even before gate truth tables are assigned. No production integration claimed.

## 3. Explicit unbounded separation from original Hall matching

Let m>=2 and D=2m+1. Data are hub x0, private x1..xm, and further data y1..ym. Create D first-layer gates:
    g0 = binary(x0,q0)
    a_i = binary(x0,x_i)
    b_i = binary(y_i,q_i), i=1..m.
Pair a_i,b_i in one binary gate per i, then combine those m results and g0 by a binary tree of m further gates. All IDs live, total D+2m=2D-1. No assignment of the gate truth tables is assumed to compute the D-way selector.

The original ancestry matching constraints pass: every nonhub data bit reaches its own distinct first-layer gate and all ancestors of that leaf in the combining tree. For any k nonhub selected leaves, the union of their root paths in the combining tree includes at least k internal vertices, because its pruned subtree on these k leaves contains at least k-1 branching vertices and at least one additional unary vertex (the selected leaves are a proper subset of all D leaves, excluding g0). Thus their total ancestry count is >=2k. Any subset containing the hub has all first-layer gates reachable from the hub only if those gates read the hub; b_i do not. More precisely, the hub reaches g0 and every a_i, and **every internal combining-tree vertex** in this chosen tree (each bottom pair contains a_i; every upper vertex combines these pairs and g0). Thus the hub already has m+1 first-layer gates plus 2m internal gates, total 3m+1. For a subset containing the hub and k_y of the y bits, add their k_y distinct b_i. Let k_x be number of selected x_i (<=m). The subset size is 1+k_x+k_y. For a proper subset, compare
    (3m+1+k_y) - 2(1+k_x+k_y) = 3m-1-2k_x-k_y >=0,
because k_x+k_y<2m and max 2k_x+k_y on that proper range is 3m-1. For full subset the ancestry count is 4m+1=2D-1. This proves all original Hall inequalities, hence all single-exempt matchings.

The sole-reader domination condition also passes: each nonhub bit has one reader, and every other data bit has a route to output avoiding that reader (the hub has g0 as a bypass). Query-only entry signals number m+1, so no simple insufficient-log-query-bit obstruction for these sizes (m+1>=ceil(log2 D)). The obvious initial query-free cut can carry D independent data bits: choose a_i=x0 XOR x_i, expose their outputs, raw x0 and all y_i. This map is injective. We do NOT claim every older semantic or cut certificate passes; the separation is from original Hall and sole-reader domination specifically, with these extra simple controls.

Fix x0, retain the selector queries for all other D-1 bits. Optimistically g0 aliases q0 and each a_i aliases x_i. Exactly m+1 counted original gates disappear; all b_i and all internal combining-tree gates remain with data ancestry. Thus remaining counted gates 3m=2D-m-2, but a (D-1)-entry selector needs 2D-3=4m-1. Deficit m-1, unbounded. This rules out every choice of truth tables for the specified topology, not merely the XOR choice used to illustrate the query-free cut. The direct-reader slack rule alone detects this particular family; the full quotient is more general and can also detect alias collisions. The example is not a hard Boolean function or a SAT lower bound, and we do not claim it is a topology generated by the production search.

## 4. Tight-budget reader rigidity and parity reassociation

Assume a circuit for a D-entry selector has exactly s=2D-1 counted gates, D>=3, OR a batched product with r>=2 groups each of size>=2 has exactly s=2D-r counted gates. The latter is a necessary-condition analysis, NOT an existence claim at that budget. In either case every single data deletion leaves a valid target whose lower bound is s-2 (including a singleton group in the batched case). Thus any valid single-bit deletion, with any legitimate substitution from the prior elimination proof and full signed simplification, cannot remove more than two original counted gate IDs or leave fewer than s-2 data-dependent gates. All counted gates live and proper.

1. Every data primary has either one or two direct reader gates (essentiality; if >=3, fixing it and avoiding its queries removes >=3). If it has one reader g, g is not output by the prior domination lemma. Forcing g constant by the permitted canalizing/parity substitution removes g and each of its distinct successors. Hence g has exactly one successor.
2. If x has two readers, BOTH must be parity gates (XOR/XNOR, including signed phases). Suppose one reader g is canalizing in x. Choose its controlling x value; both readers disappear under simplification and g becomes constant. It cannot be output because the retained target is nonconstant in other data. Any successor of g outside the two readers would be a third eliminated gate. Thus g feeds only the other reader h, which also directly reads x. Since these are proper binary gates, h computes a Boolean function of x and g(x,u), i.e. a function of just x and the other operand u of g. Replace this two-gate composition by at most one binary gate (or unary/constant) and redirect h's output, with no new paths except contraction of existing ones. Because g has no other successor, this preserves the entire original target with strictly fewer than s counted data-dependent gates, contradicting the lower bound. Thus no canalizing reader is possible. Every proper nonparity truth table is canalizing in each operand.
3. If x has one reader g and g is parity, its unique successor h must also be parity: otherwise force g to the controlling value of h by substituting the appropriate value of g's other operand for x (retained queries ignore x). Both g and h become constant. If h is output the retained target is incorrectly constant, otherwise a third successor gate disappears. Now reassociate the two parity gates, absorbing signs:
        h=(x XOR u) XOR v  -->  x XOR (u XOR v).
   Since g had only successor h, v cannot depend on x or g; u also cannot depend on x. The rewrite is acyclic, uses at most the same two gates and does not increase counted data gates; all downstream outputs are unchanged. If the new first gate is query-only or collapsible, the total count decreases, contradicting the lower bound. Otherwise it still has exactly s counted gates and x now has sole reader h, also parity. The same necessity applies again. Each reassociation advances the sole reader to a later vertex on a downstream path whose subsequent edges are unchanged; a finite acyclic graph cannot continue this indefinitely, and an output reader violates domination. Contradiction. Hence a sole data reader is CANALIZING.
4. For a sole canalizing reader g, let c be its forced constant at its controlling x value. Its unique successor h must not become constant merely from that operand value c: otherwise a third removal (or constant output) contradicts tightness. The other operand u of g is either query-only or a branching counted gate (at least two distinct successors). It cannot be another raw data primary: if that other bit has two readers, item 2 requires g parity; if it has one, two sole-reader bits sharing g violates the domination lemma because every pair of original data bits has an allowed query retaining one while avoiding the other (for batched product choose other entries in the avoided bit's group, set selected others to 1). It cannot be a counted gate with g as its only successor, because fixing x to its controlling value makes g constant and prunes that side operand as a third counted removal. This also proves every gate reading two raw data bits in a tight circuit is a parity gate with both data bits twice-read.

For arbitrary slack sigma, every original data bit has at most 2+sigma readers, and every sole reader has at most 1+sigma distinct successors, by the same deletion budget. The parity classification above is asserted only at sigma=0 with the stated deletion-stability premise. In particular D=2 for a single selector is excluded: the usual three-gate XOR mux has a sole parity reader and its deletion leaves one bit with zero gate lower bound, allowing three removals. Do not extrapolate the exact-budget rules to that case or to AND-count models.

## 5. Constant-seed cascades and saturated branching at batched equality

**Cascade lemma.** Let k pairwise ancestor-incomparable live gates be forced constant simultaneously in an original proper binary DAG, and suppose the output remains nonconstant. Let P be the closure of these seeds under gates whose two operands are already in P (ignore all additional opportunities from controlling values). Let U be the set of gates outside P with exactly one operand in P. Each gate of P and U is guaranteed to collapse to a constant or unary; count their original IDs even if later dead pruning would remove some. Since the output is not in P, each vertex of P has at least one original outgoing wire. Every such wire goes to P or U. No seed has an operand in P, by pairwise incomparability and construction of P. Thus the number of these wires is exactly 2(|P|-k)+|U| and at least |P|. Rearranging,
    |P|-k+|U| >= k.
So at least k additional distinct gates collapse beyond the k constant seeds. This counts only an elementary constant propagation closure; actual controlling-value cascades, equality aliases and dead cones can add more. This is not a claim of k additional removals for arbitrary comparable seed sets. The DAG may reconverge: a merge of two constant seeds creates a new constant, and the outgoing wire count accounts for the next collapse without double counting.

Now consider the hypothetical tight batched circuit s=2D-r, r>=2, all groups size>=2. Let b be the number of twice-read data bits, Q the number of query-only operand occurrences entering counted gates, e the sum of outdegree-minus-one over nonoutput counted gates (internal fanout excess). The internal operand wire count is s-1+e; direct data occurrences D+b. Count all 2s operand slots:
    s=D-1+b+Q+e.
Each of the D-b sole-reader bits has a distinct reader g_x, with side operand either a query-only signal or a branching counted gate by Section 4. For each branching counted vertex v let c_v be its number of outgoing wires to these sole-reader gates as their side operand. Call v SATURATED if all its outgoing wires have that form; write H for the set of saturated branching vertices. If v not saturated, c_v<=outdeg(v)-1; if saturated, c_v=outdeg(v). Consequently
    D-b <= Q+e+|H|
and therefore
    s>=2D-1-|H|,  |H|>=r-1.
In particular any circuit meeting the old batched lower bound for r>=2 must reconverge in the counted data-dependent DAG. A read-once internal tree (even allowing repeated raw data and arbitrary query-only decoding) cannot attain it. For two groups this excludes the one-gate-below-upper budget in the subclass of circuits without internal data-gate fanout. It does NOT exclude general DAGs or establish exact unrestricted B2 complexity.

Stronger structure for v in H: let X_v be the set of sole data bits whose readers are its successors. They are pairwise distinct, their readers have no edges among them because each reads only its bit and v; in fact they are pairwise ancestor-incomparable (one cannot reach another except through a raw primary or v, impossible in the DAG). |X_v|=outdeg(v)>=2. The X_v for distinct saturated vertices are disjoint (each sole reader has only one side input).

Suppose X_v avoids containing an entire group, and deleting all of X_v would leave at least one nonsingleton group. Fix each bit to the controlling value of its own reader, retaining queries avoiding X_v. This makes all k=|X_v| successor readers constant simultaneously. The constant-seed cascade lemma removes at least k more counted gates downstream. Vertex v itself is upstream of all seeds and has no other successors, so it is dead and gives one more distinct removal. Thus at least 2k+1 counted gates disappear. But the retained target still has lower bound s-2k, contradiction. All collapse charges are original IDs; no summation over different saturated sets.

Therefore EVERY saturated branch set X_v must satisfy one of:
    (i) it contains an entire original table group;
    (ii) it contains all but one entry of EVERY original group (and no entire group).
The second exception is essential: if all surviving groups become singleton, the core lower bound drops by the extra one. Because the X_v are disjoint, if exception (ii) occurs, no other X_w can contain an entire group, and a second exception (ii) is possible only if every group has exactly two entries (it must be the complementary transversal). Thus for r>=4, or r>=3 with some group size>=3, the requirement |H|>=r-1 rules out the exceptional type altogether. In those regimes at least r-1 saturated branch sets must capture distinct entire groups. These are necessary structural constraints on circuits at the old lower bound, not a proof such circuits cannot exist. Do not infer a new unconditional lower bound from them.

## 7. Scope and next target

The optimistic quotient plus old matching is a practical new topology pruning rule. It detects an unbounded gate deficit missed by the original ancestry matching, but the deficit is on deliberately bad candidate DAGs, not an unrestricted numerical complexity lower bound. The equality-budget cascade proof yields genuine extra structural conditions, including necessary reconvergence and capture of whole groups by saturated branches. It does not yet close the general batched gap of r-1. For the packed SAT applications the old 2rM-r and 2m*ell-1 numerical ancestry bounds remain the applicable results; no new physical grammar or replay. A search engineer could test a modest collection of deletion masks and update alias quotients incrementally; checking all masks may be exponential, and no performance benchmark or production implementation is asserted. Independent proof audit and Lean formalization outstanding.

Next: investigate the structure forced by a saturated branch feeding every entry of a full group, particularly whether independent query choices force another unavoidable cascade or additional live gate. Try to close the 2D-1 vs 2D-r data-dependent B2 gap without arbitrary nonlocal query substitutions; use one-hot implementations as controls. More importantly for L-clb-036, look for harder restrictions or cascades that enforce a growing average charge; the present selector families have linear/quasilinear upper bounds and cannot by themselves yield a superlinear numerical result. Do not sum charges over different deletion masks. No upstream submission. L-clb-036 remains open.

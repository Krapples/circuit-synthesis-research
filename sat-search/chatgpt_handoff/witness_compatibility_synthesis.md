Thus the earlier handoff's "outstanding cubic grammar replay" and "unaudited sparse routing" statuses are superseded. The native unsupplied marginal cubic cover algorithm gives O((n+3^h+2^(h+t)) polylog(n)) size. Do not duplicate these tasks. Native robust SAT lower bound is already Lean checked at multiples of six, near 2n in the external counted model; general all-even tails remain separate. This is not superlinear.

Our previous results eliminate a succession of possible proxies for distinct gate complexity: near-cubic formula complexity and distributed high paths; maximal residual diversity on logarithmic probes (earlier archive); arbitrary permutation of source chunks; exponential witness multiplicity and affine coupling; quadratic degree; disconnected cubic components; bounded-degree large connected cubic graphs with tractable exceptional marginals; and selected high-degree hub regimes. Counting cubic characters is #P-hard, but deciding image support can still be easy. These controls concern different functions and promises, and none alone establishes a full SAT lower bound.

There is a structural pattern: every single-address lookup has a semantic bottleneck of k=log_2 M output bits, where the full table of M bits is already part of the input. Enumerating all addresses, or performing Walsh transforms over them, fits the budget. Independent compression has unusually favorable economics here. When a system instead contains r separately programmed local checks with shared existential variables, their combined output dimension is the sum of the individual widths, even if every individual width is one. An exact local image can lose all correlations. The native quadratic witness note proves that conjunctions of one-bit quadratic checks already express arbitrary 3-CNF via unique auxiliary extensions. This is the more promising change of research object, rather than simply increasing the degree of one global address map.

But global compatibility alone is also not a sufficient lower-bound measure. The new theorem below tests this by combining exact marginal blindness with the strongest packed formula/path control so far.

## 2. A simultaneous negative control: high formula complexity plus total proper-marginal blindness

The graph-parity idea is the classical Tseitin construction, not new. On a connected graph with r vertices and e edges, its binary incidence matrix B has rank r-1: the row sum is zero, and a spanning tree allows solving any even charge vector by leaf elimination. Equivalently the only row dependence is the all-ones vector. See e.g. Itsykson, Riazanov and Smirnov, Tight Bounds for Tseitin Formulas, SAT 2022, https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SAT.2022.6. Our application couples this familiar parity relation to the actual permutable packed Andreev grammar. It is a negative control for combining several earlier research proxies, not a claim of a new Tseitin result.

For each edge introduce three independent free witness bits x_e,y_e,z_e and let its effective edge bit be

    w_e = x_e XOR (y_e AND z_e).

The vector w is uniform on GF(2)^e, since each effective bit has exactly four preimages. For a charge vector b in GF(2)^r the local residual map is

    R_b(x,y,z) = B w XOR b.

It has degree at most two. Its image is the coset of the even-parity subspace with parity sum b. Each attained residual vector has exactly 2^(3e-r+1) free-witness assignments. Hence the constraints requiring all local residuals zero are jointly satisfiable iff parity(b)=0. For EVERY proper subset S of the r residual coordinates, the projection of either parity coset is all GF(2)^S with uniform multiplicity. More explicitly each projected value has exactly 2^(3e-|S|) preimages, regardless of b. Thus even an oracle giving the complete exact histograms of ALL proper-subset residual maps, indexed by their subset, cannot distinguish the two charge parities. This is an information-theoretic failure of a precisely specified abstraction; a solver also allowed to inspect b can simply take its parity. The statement uses residuals including the required charges, so "all constraints satisfied" always corresponds to the same target zero vector.

Now let M=2^k, and use the prior permutable packed Andreev projection with m=kJ=Theta(M/k) full selector chunks. Replace the last data check by an output variable a recording the selected table bit. Specifically, in the old guard layout replace every final clause (-g_i OR V_p) with

    (-g_i OR -a OR V_p), (-g_i OR a OR -V_p).

Exactly one high-address guard is active. Its V_p value is forced by the low address and its pattern, so these clauses force a=h[address]. The two copies of the pattern digits occupy fixed disjoint coordinates; selector digits still have their original two copies. Choose a cycle of r=max(3,m) vertices (or any fixed connected bounded-degree graph with Theta(M/k) vertices). The graph's first vertex has charge a; the other vertices have independently programmed charge bits c_1,...,c_(r-1), each encoded by one unit polarity. The graph constraint at a cycle vertex is the XOR of its two incident effective edge variables equaling its charge. Encode each edge using three free bits, one AND auxiliary and one XOR auxiliary. Encode each vertex XOR by four standard clauses. This is a unique extension of the free witness bits before checking the vertex constraints (on the used variables). All old and new auxiliaries fit in one disjoint common-width ID interval: the old count A plus 1+(r-1)+5r for the cycle. Choose Q as the next power of two at least max(4P,A+6r). All such identifiers have O(k) bits. The added O(r) gadgets and O(M/k) extra data-clause literals preserve length Theta(M), constant across permutations, h, x and charge bits. The earlier exact padding and signed balancing work unchanged. The actual new packed word generator is exercised below.

The SAT predicate on this entire promise is

    SAT(h,x,permutation,c) = 1 iff h[address(x,permutation)] XOR parity(c) = 0.

Take c of odd parity. The exact original packed Andreev function is recovered, with the same source occurrence count for selectors and a bounded constant count for data. Tal's revised composition lemma (TR14-048 revision 1, Lemma 7.1, https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/048/revision/1/) and the reconfigurable light-chunk theorem from [permutable_andreev_handoff.md](permutable_andreev_handoff.md) apply unchanged, including the all-sufficiently-large-even-length padding argument. Therefore the coupled promise simultaneously has:

- De Morgan formula leaf lower bound Omega(n^3/(log^2 n log log n)).
- Omega(n) distinct primary high-path obligations; for size s, Omega(n) internal gates with path count Omega(n^3/(s log^2 n log log n)).
- Theta(n/log n) coupled one-bit local quadratic checks, with every proper subset of their joint residual histogram exactly identical between an accepted and a rejected charge pattern. In particular the minimum order of a marginal that can see the difference is the entire collection of checks.
- Positive instances with exponentially many free-witness assignments, exactly 2^(3e-r+1) in the fixed connected graph, and zero for negatives. For the cycle e=r.
- Equal-length accepted/rejected words differing in a single charge polarity bit, including at any sufficiently large padded even length. On the balanced Hamming layer that one change is copied with opposite phase.

Nevertheless the whole variable-permutation, variable-data, variable-charge promise has a deterministic O(n log^2 n)-size signed AIG, by the existing packed sort-and-lookup algorithm followed by parity(c) and equality. A circuit may ignore the graph's existential bits; they are not inputs to the SAT-deciding circuit. This is a paper upper bound for the promise only. The control remains even if the fixed connected graph is an expander, for which Tseitin refutation hardness in various restricted proof systems is known. The easy parity-of-charges SAT decision does not contradict those proof lower bounds.

Thus any proposed universal gate-count lower-bound principle based solely on the conjunction of the above properties would be false. It does not refute a more selective, circuit-sensitive argument that also accounts for how the correlations are represented and shared. The classical graph parity fact and packed Andreev bound are imported explicitly; no claim of novelty relative to all literature.

## 4. Three ways to reinterpret the program

### A. From local compression to exact boundary relations

The right compositional object for a subformula with private witnesses y and shared boundary variables u is the relation R(u)=exists y F(u,y), not the Boolean value exists u,y F. Joining two subregions means intersecting their boundary relations on common assignments (or taking a natural join when their boundaries differ). For arbitrary future pinning contexts, two subregions are interchangeable iff their boundary relations coincide: if they differ at u, append pins fixing u. Every subset of {0,1}^b is realizable as a relation on b boundary variables (forbid every excluded assignment by one clause). Therefore an arbitrary exact context-independent summary requires at least 2^b bits in the worst case, by counting. The observation is a standard residual/one-way communication argument, not a new unrestricted circuit bound. Our earlier universal residual-family examples show why even saturated semantic interface capacity is consistent with quasi-linear circuits: a compact program or an interactive decoder may answer queries instead of materializing a relation separately for every subregion.

Interpretation: look for the cost of REPEATEDLY changing the context, with shared boundary summaries that cannot all be implemented by the same small gate set. Do not count relation sizes independently. A possible practical program is to record exact boundary relations on small coupled SAT instances, use the native orientation constructor and mandatory-query-interface extraction to measure the gate-cone union, and compare the marginal cost of adding contexts against the cost of each standalone query. Native NOTES_ORIENTATION_QUOTIENT explicitly warns that marginal costs can undercut standalone support bounds through sharing. Require negative controls (the construction in Section 2, earlier universal residual family, affine and quadratic compressible cases). A statistic that declares them superlinear has failed.

### B. From path multiplicity to a restriction ledger

The strongest existing lower bounds in the repo charge actual gate deletion, not counts of unfolded paths. Gate elimination is also the classical general-circuit technique; Li and Yang's 3.1n-o(n) B2 lower bound for affine dispersers is a useful point of comparison, https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2021/023/ (different basis from signed AIGs). Two 2026 papers investigate constructivity of gate elimination: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23958 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17942. The limitation of simple gate elimination is already studied in Iwama and Morizumi's line of work; see "On the limits of gate elimination", https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000018305166. The exact scopes matter: these are not theorems that all restrictions or all circuit methods fail.

A concrete falsifiable direction: for a proposed universal SAT circuit C and a finite family of legal packed restrictions rho_j, keep stable original gate IDs, record deleted-gate sets D_j and surviving cone sets L_j, and also semantic obligations (boundary separations/derivatives) after substitution. Try to derive a multi-context inequality that forces low overlap of surviving gate roles, rather than simply summing |L_j| or p_j(g). If a fractional demand packing can assign nonnegative weights to obligations with a proved per-original-gate capacity, its dual lower bound is a genuine distinct-gate charge. The difficulty is the universal capacity lemma: a gate can serve many contexts, so it must be justified by circuit semantics, not imposed as a heuristic. An efficient implementation of the ledger on known circuits and a SAT search for counterexamples at small n is a worthwhile first step. The existing native restriction counters and mandatory interface tools are suitable for this, but their current guarantees do not supply such a lemma. Do not promote a heuristic to a pruning rule.

### C. From a hard SAT instance to a hard family of decisions

Proof complexity distinguishes a refutation of one unsatisfiable formula from a circuit deciding all formula encodings. Tseitin demonstrates the distinction especially well: some proof systems have exponential refutation lower bounds on expander instances, while deciding satisfiability for the fixed graph/variable charge promise is parity. Similarly, hard exact cubic counts do not imply hard image decision. The right target is a family in which incidence and constraints jointly vary and no known global normal form (rank, parity, small kernel, shared lookup) computes the changing decision.

The Karchmer-Wigderson connection translates circuit depth into communication; DAG-like communication can represent sharing (e.g. https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2016/202/). Monotone lifting results do not automatically apply to arbitrary signed circuits, nor to the guarded Hamming slice where dual rails can simulate negations. An exploratory program could try a SAT-specific search relation on pairs of opposite-satisfiability encodings and explicitly compare tree versus DAG protocols. The relevant challenge is unrestricted DAG size, not merely communication depth. Use the joint-witness control above as a falsifier.

The Williams algorithms-to-lower-bounds perspective (https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/acc-lbs-ccc.pdf and https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/projects.html) is another important distinction. Our compression algorithms are upper bounds for structured SAT INPUT promises, not satisfiability algorithms for arbitrary circuits from a candidate circuit CLASS. They cannot simply be inserted into that framework. A more ambitious route is to assume a small general SAT circuit, exploit structural constraints on its own representation to accelerate a circuit-analysis algorithm, and use an appropriate hierarchy theorem. No such reduction or savings theorem has been obtained here.

Natural Proofs (Razborov-Rudich, https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/1994/010/) and locality barriers in hardness magnification (https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2019/168/) are useful warnings against generic truth-table statistics or unexamined oracle gates. They do not directly prohibit the target n^1.01 bound for this explicit SAT encoding; check hypotheses before invoking them. Haramaty-Shpilka's cubic structure theorem (https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2853) motivates continued algebraic work, but high-bias decompositions with many forms and expensive search are not automatically near-linear circuits.

## 5. Suggested next milestone, not a promised breakthrough

Prioritize a small multi-context gate-role experiment over another ever more complicated single-address upper bound. Build a matched suite of variable-incidence shared quadratic checks (including the native 3-CNF reduction), the packed Andreev parity control, and ordinary SAT constraints. Keep exact grammar lengths and original gate IDs comparable. Run known small-circuit constructions and exact synthesis where feasible. Extract boundary relations and per-context survivor/reader information; search for a simple capacity lemma that survives both the easy controls and all enumerated circuits. Reject any proposed lemma with a small counterexample, and only formalize a sound inequality in Lean after proof. A falsifiable near-term goal is an independently validated multi-context inequality improving the native gate-elimination bound on a finite family; this is not equivalent to L-clb-036 but would move toward the missing bridge.

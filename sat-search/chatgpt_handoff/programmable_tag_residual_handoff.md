# Programmable unit-tag residual diversity and disjoint core-reserve gate charges

Date: 2026-11-26. Continues [packed unit-clause separator construction](packed_separator_handoff.md), [binary selector elimination](binary_selector_elimination_handoff.md), and [batched selector gap](batched_selector_tight_gap_handoff.md). Pivot from independently addressed products to the larger programmable unit-clause promise. New exact residual-count theorem, subset-wise distinct binary-gate allocation, and an asymptotically stronger payload-ancestry bound for the existing physical SAT encoding. Paper proofs and exact finite regression, not independent audit or Lean verification. No production changes, new physical packing, novelty claim, or unrestricted superlinear lower bound. L-clb-036 remains open.

## 1. Reuse the exact physical unit-clause layout

Use the layout in packed_separator_handoff.md, Section 2: m=2^k slots, ell=floor(k/4)>=1, P=2^ell, Q the least power of two >=(m+1)P, width w=log2(Q)+1. There are P dictionary unit clauses at tag 0, whose signs specify an arbitrary Boolean h on ell-bit patterns. Slot i has identifier Q+tag_i*P+x_i and sign s_i. Every pattern bit is a single independent physical primary, with fixed positions (P+i)(2w+6)+3+2j. Tags, signs and dictionary signs are metadata, disjoint from the payloads. Word length n_k=(P+m)(2w+6); D=m ell designated data bits. All IDs are canonical fixed width. This round uses the prior fixed tautology padding proof for all sufficiently large even lengths; no new padding replay.

In the old pair mode each nonzero tag was used for at most two active slots. More generally use one nonzero tag for a set of slots, with a nontrivial split of signs. Its exact consistency condition is
    AND_{i,j with equal tag and opposite sign} [x_i != x_j].
Thus every nontrivial connected component of the conflict graph can be an arbitrary complete bipartite graph on its labeled slots. Different components get different nonzero tags; isolated slots get unused distinct tags. This uses at most m nonzero tags. The dictionary at tag 0 is consistent and irrelevant when no slot uses tag 0. All of these words are legal for every assignment of all pattern bits, so the metadata can vary on one common data cube. We consider only this graph subfamily, not arbitrary graphs with overlapping biclique constraints. The full promise still has a quasi-linear upper circuit by sorting the unit identifiers and checking for opposite signs; it is not claimed hard in unrestricted circuit size.

## 2. Exact graph residual count on heterogeneous free coordinates

Let S be any nonempty subset of the D physical payload bits, write s=|S|, s_i=# of S bits in slot i, and a=#slots with s_i>0. Fix all payload bits outside S to zero, while keeping S fully free. All metadata are query inputs and the allowed domain can be any chosen collection of legal metadata words independent of S.

For any nonempty set J of occupied slots (s_i>0), program a graph G on J with connected components either isolated vertices or nontrivial complete bipartite graphs. Make every other slot inactive with its own unused tag. Denote the resulting residual F_G=AND_{ij edge G}[x_i != x_j] on the retained payloads in J.

Claim: the F_G are pairwise distinct, nonzero functions, even if every slot retains only ONE bit and the retained bit coordinates differ between slots.

Proof of nonzero: for each nontrivial bipartite component choose one side and give every vertex on it the all-zero pattern; give every vertex of the other side a nonzero pattern, using any one of its free bits. All edge endpoints then differ. Isolated vertices arbitrary. For distinction choose an edge uv of G absent in G'. In G' the two vertices are either in different components, or in the same component on the same bipartition side (or isolated). Choose orientations of their components so that both receive zero; give all opposite-side vertices a nonzero pattern. This satisfies every G' edge but fails the G edge uv. Thus F_G differs from F_G'. This works without requiring the nonzero patterns of different slots to be equal. Fixing other free coordinates within a slot to zero is allowed just for the distinguishing evaluation. The residuals themselves are defined on the full free data cube for J.

Let a_j denote the number of such labeled graphs on j vertices (use a_0=1). The graph itself, not the arbitrary tag names or global swaps of signs within a component, is counted. Its component containing a designated vertex is either isolated or has b>=2 vertices, b-1 others chosen from j-1. With the designated vertex on the first side, choose a nonempty subset of the other b-1 vertices for the opposite side: 2^(b-1)-1 choices. Each gives a unique complete bipartite connected graph because its bipartition is unique up to swapping sides. Remaining components are independent. Hence exact recurrence
    a_j = a_(j-1) + sum_{b=2}^j binom(j-1,b-1) (2^(b-1)-1) a_(j-b).
Initial values a_0..a_6 = 1,1,2,7,29,136,737.
Thus exact count of distinct residuals of this graph subfamily = a_j, not a count of tag assignments.

Asymptotics:
    log2 a_m = m log2 m - O(m log log m)
in the sense of lower bound m log m-O(m log log m) and upper bound m log m+O(m), so log2 a_m=(1-o(1)) m log2 m. For lower, take block size b=floor(log2 m)>=2, g=floor(m/b), N=gb, fix m-N designated isolated leftovers, partition N labeled vertices into g unlabeled b-sets and choose an arbitrary nontrivial bipartition in each. At least
    N! / ((b!)^g g!) * (2^(b-1)-1)^g
different graphs. Using log N! >= N log N-O(N) (integral bound), b!<=b^b, g!<=g^g, and N=m-O(log m), gives m log m-O(m log log m) even without the bipartition factors. Upper: assign each vertex an arbitrary one of m labels and a sign, at most m^m 2^m assignments, covering all graphs. No precise second-order asymptotic or literature priority claimed.

## 3. More residuals by reserving a dictionary slot

For a chosen c in J, program all other J slots as above (nonzero tags), but give c tag 0, sign chosen to demand h(x_c)=1. Since outside-S payload bits of c are fixed to zero, the dictionary h can specify ANY Boolean function on its s_c independent bits, by assigning those 2^s_c dictionary entries arbitrarily and filling other entries consistently. Select nonzero local predicates only; there are 2^(2^s_c)-1 of them. This can be done independently of the graph on J minus c, because its slots use nonzero tags.

The resulting conjunctions are all distinct and nonzero: each is a product of nonzero factors on disjoint variable sets, so the projections of its nonempty satisfying set recover the two factor satisfying sets uniquely. Hence a certified residual-count lower bound for a fixed core J is
    R(J)=max(a_|J|, max_{c in J} a_(|J|-1) * (2^(2^s_c)-1)).
We take max, not sum: the two families can overlap. The true count over all legal metadata may be larger. For singleton J={c}, arbitrary h (including zero) actually gives 2^(2^s_c) residuals and exact logarithmic requirement 2^s_c. Since s_c>=1, ceil(log2(2^(2^s_c)-1))=2^s_c anyway. More than one independent dictionary slot can sometimes be realized using disjoint pattern subcubes, but no such additional count is needed here or asserted.

## 4. Operand-slot lemma: combine data support and query residual count without double charges

Consider any circuit of proper binary Boolean gates, with constants, signed wires and fanout free; arbitrary query-only preprocessing allowed and uncharged. Work in the live simplified cone, count B data-dependent gate IDs (syntactic ancestry in a designated set of d independent data bits). All d data bits essential in at least one allowed query. Assume the target is not just a raw data bit or its negation (in our application, query choices include both selection phases, so the output must be a counted gate). Define Q_D to be the number of operand occurrences in counted gates sourced from vertices with NO data ancestry (query-only signals, including constants if present). Let I be the number of operand occurrences directly from data primaries, and E be the number of operand occurrences between counted gates. Count slots:
    2B=I+E+Q_D.
Each essential data primary occurs at least once, I>=d. Every counted gate other than the output has at least one outgoing counted edge, so E>=B-1. All query-only sources can be treated as arbitrary functions of the query; their Q_D bits and all the data primaries suffice to evaluate the entire counted cone in topological order. Consequently if the target has N distinct data residual functions over allowed queries, N<=2^Q_D. This gives the necessary lower bound
    B >= d-1+ceil(log2 N).
Stronger ledger for specified topology: if p distinct query-only source vertices (underlying signals, ignoring phase) feed the counted cone, then p>=ceil(log2 N), Q_D>=p. More precisely define e_data=I-d>=0 and e_internal=E-(B-1)>=0; then
    B=d-1+Q_D+e_data+e_internal.
This is an exact wire-slot identity, not a sum of multiple complexity bounds. The query capacity is ordinary deterministic residual counting, not matrix/translation rank. It is valid when query-only sources have arbitrary fanout and repeated occurrences; repetitions cannot increase the number of residuals. Free signs do not add independent bits.

For any S in the physical SAT DAG, all S bits are essential somewhere in the above promise: choose dictionary h(x)=the desired bit coordinate, activate just its slot at tag 0 demanding that bit, make other slots inactive. The opposite sign also exposes its complement. This remains true after fixing outside-S payloads to zero, and the selector metadata can be chosen separately for each bit. Treat every non-S physical input as query or fixed constants. Restriction and simplification cannot increase original ancestry, so the bound counts distinct original gate IDs reachable from S. No lower bound on query-only gate count itself is implied, since their sources could be raw metadata primaries.

## 5. New cross-restriction core-reserve theorem

For every S and every nonempty J of its occupied slots, let d_J=sum_{i in J}s_i, and let A(S) be the union of original live proper binary gate IDs descended from S. Then
    |A(S)| >= 2(s-d_J) + d_J - 1 + ceil(log2 R(J))
            = 2s-d_J-1+ceil(log2 R(J)).
Also |A(S)|>=2s-1 for s>=2, from the ordinary singleton-selector subfamily (and the stronger phase-complete tight-budget obstruction when s>=3, see below). Maximize the certified core-reserve bound over nonempty J, rather than adding different core choices.

Proof: fix all physical payload bits outside S to zero. Retain a common allowed metadata domain containing (i) the graph core and one-dictionary-slot core contexts on J used to certify R(J), with every slot outside J inactive, and (ii) a singleton-selector context of either phase for every bit of S. The latter contexts use dictionary h equal to the selected coordinate or its complement through the unit sign, activate only its slot, and leave all other slots inactive. Every core context ignores ALL data bits of S outside J, even when those bits are set to arbitrary functions of other data/query inputs; their slots are inactive. Delete these s-d_J bits one at a time. At each deletion the erased bit is essential in its own selector context; retain all core contexts and all selector contexts for other surviving bits, which ignore the erased bit globally. At least one surviving bit remains essential. The rank-free two-gate deletion theorem from binary_selector_elimination_handoff.md therefore removes at least two distinct original counted binary gates per step. If it uses a parity-reader substitution depending on other inputs, this is legitimate on the retained legal promise (all payload assignments are allowed and the target ignores that bit); no new circuit gates are inserted. The earlier proof tracks stable IDs and never introduces new original ancestry.

After these deletions the remaining d_J bits are all essential at selector contexts and the remaining circuit realizes >=R(J) distinct residuals. Apply the operand-slot lemma to that circuit: at least d_J-1+ceil(log2 R(J)) surviving counted gates are required. These IDs are disjoint from all IDs removed in the deletion process, and all are descendants of S in the original graph. This proves the combined reserve bound. The combination is stronger than simply taking independent maxima in some regimes, because the deleted and surviving IDs are explicitly disjoint. It does NOT delete unused coordinates within active graph slots by a parity substitution: the graph predicate could depend on those bits. Only whole noncore occupied slots are eliminated. No summation across different S or J is licensed.

Example core J={c}, s_c=t:
    |A(S)| >= 2s + 2^t - t - 1.
In particular every fully free physical payload slot c individually has >=2^ell+ell-1 original descendant gate IDs, because that slot alone can be queried against every Boolean dictionary. For an S with many occupied slots, taking J equal all of them gives s-1+ceil(log2 a_a). The theorem permits selecting J to exploit large dictionary residual diversity or high graph diversity while spending two gates per discarded data bit. At s=1, the one-slot dictionary term is 2, valid because residuals include x, NOT x, 0,1, not merely one projection. At s>=3, the prior exact selector normal form also implies |A(S)|>=2s: if equality 2s-1 held for the full set of positive singleton selection contexts, its counted DAG would be a one-hot tree with a common data-control baseline c. That same DAG produces the constant c at the all-c data assignment for EVERY metadata context (all sole readers are forced constant), contradicting a negative singleton context selecting NOT x_i. This additional bound depends on the earlier normal-form proof; the core-reserve bound needs only the simpler two-gate elimination theorem.

## 6. Full-payload asymptotics and limitations

Take S all D=m ell bits and J all m slots. Graph diversity alone gives
    |A(S)| >= D-1+ceil(log2 a_m)
             >= D + m log2 m - O(m log log m)
             = (5-o(1)) D  [ell=floor(k/4), m=2^k].
This improves the earlier generic singleton-selector allocation 2D-1 for this same physical promise, asymptotically by a factor approaching at least 2.5 relative to that particular bound. It does NOT improve every existing SAT lower bound, and no exact B2 complexity is claimed. The full physical word length n_k=(P+m)(2w+6)=(5/2+o(1))mk, w=k+ell+2, D=(1/4+o(1))mk. Thus the new payload-ancestry requirement is (1/2-o(1))n_k along the unpadded packed lengths. Padding to larger lengths preserves a linear bound with a possibly different constant, not necessarily 1/2. This is still only a linear lower bound in the physical input length. Query metadata itself occupies Theta(m log m) physical bits, so the residual entropy cannot be used to pretend the input length is just m or D without accounting for the metadata. The full fixed-width unit-clause promise has the known O(n log^2 n) sorting-based circuit, so it cannot directly witness the target n^1.01. The arbitrary grouping construction is a richer constraint family within the old promise, not a genuinely superlinear-hard restriction.

For a topology with total s_total counted gates, per-slot |A(slot)|>=P+ell-1 implies sum of slot-ancestor multiplicities over original gates >=m(P+ell-1). This means large overlap is NECESSARY in small circuits; it is not a license to count the same gate once per slot. For example a shared dictionary lookup after selecting the active slot can reuse gates across every slot context. The core-reserve theorem solves disjoint charging ONLY for a single chosen deletion/core sequence. Obtaining a superlinear total bound would require a new argument controlling such shared computation in a harder SAT promise, not larger residual counts alone. Indeed the log residual count across any data/metadata split is at most the number of varying metadata bits, and this slot lemma's target-only bound is therefore at most linear in total physical variables even before considering upper constructions. No claim that the graph constraints prove a stronger unrestricted SAT exponent.

## 8. Next research focus

This is a direct application of our elimination machinery to a richer reprogrammable SAT family with fixed physical payload coordinates. It adds simultaneous constraints for arbitrary subsets, and avoids invalid addition of separately measured cone bounds. It also makes the limitation visible: arbitrary group metadata is large, the entire promise is easy by sorting, and heavy reuse of downstream gates can satisfy all per-slot demands. Independent audit and Lean formalization outstanding. No production changes or upstream submission.

For the main L-clb-036 route, next examine a fixed physical layout with programmable incidence of genuine existential constraints (not unit consistency or deterministic dictionary propagation), so no known sorting/evaluation upper bound automatically caps the chosen restriction. A specific possible next step is a single-copy local-clause template whose metadata connects free payloads to shared unforced variables; first prove exact semantics and length, then look for an obstruction to small unrestricted circuits, not merely NP-hardness or a communication rank bound. The burden of a superlinear lower bound remains, and no such result is established here. Avoid calling independent linear gates per slot additive or interpreting logarithmic residual entropy as a superlinear bound in the full word length.

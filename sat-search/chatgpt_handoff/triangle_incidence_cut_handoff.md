## 1. Exact single-copy triangle incidence

Let A=2^b, b>=1. Take three independent Boolean A-by-A matrices X,Y,Z. Define
    T(X,Y,Z) = OR_{i,j,k} (X_ij AND Y_jk AND Z_ki).
This is tripartite triangle existence. Use the existing switchable layout, with m the least power of two >=3A^2+9(A-1)+4, P=16A, ell=log2 P=b+4, and the previous width/namespace definitions. All physical metadata fixed: hard mode p=0, every programmable tag 1, positive physical signs, unused dictionary all zero. Logical signed witness literal indices are 2v (positive) or 2v+1 (negative) as before.

Allocate logical witness vertices a_i=i, b_j=A+j, c_k=2A+k, f=3A. Pin f=false by the triple (NOT f,NOT f,NOT f). Emit one logical triple for each potential edge in all three arrays, in fixed layer-major lexicographic order:
    (NOT a_i, NOT b_j, f or NOT f according to X_ij);
    (NOT b_j, NOT c_k, f or NOT f according to Y_jk);
    (NOT c_k, NOT a_i, f or NOT f according to Z_ki).
Each third literal has signed witness index 2f+edge_bit. Thus each graph edge bit is exactly the low physical payload digit (digit 0) of that slot, with positive orientation and single occurrence. All other index digits, slots and metadata are constants. An absent edge (bit zero) forbids simultaneous selection of its endpoints, a present edge (bit one) makes the constraint true. No additional variable per edge.

Require at least one selected vertex in each layer. For each layer compute a sequential OR of its A witness variables, with A-1 fresh existential auxiliary variables. For y=u OR v emit (NOT u OR y), (NOT v OR y), (NOT y OR u OR v), padding the binary clauses with positive f. Pin the final OR true by (last, f, f). Three layers use 9(A-1)+3 groups, plus the f pin, plus the 3A^2 edge groups. At most 6A-2 logical variables, within P/2=8A. Pad unused groups with (f,NOT f,f). All signed indices fit the fixed ell digits. The existing switchable equivalence for existential guard assignments then implements these logical triples exactly.

If the graph has a triangle, select its three vertices and set all other vertices false, compute the OR witnesses and extend complement/guard variables. All edge constraints hold. Conversely any satisfying assignment selects at least one vertex in each layer, and every cross-layer selected pair has its edge present, so taking one selected vertex from each layer gives a triangle. No exact-one constraints needed. The witness choices are not pinned by address metadata. All D=3A^2 independent edge bits may vary simultaneously. The positions are fixed across ALL graph inputs and every cofactor below; no movable role placement. Physical word length n=(3P+15m+1)(2w+4)+2(2P+7m+1)=Theta(A^2 log A), D=Theta(n/log n). No new padding replay. The proof uses a specific parameter choice within the existing grammar, not the previous triple-lookup word lengths.

## 2. Arbitrary-cut matching theorem

Color every one of the D edge coordinates by its side of an arbitrary bipartition, colors 0,1. Let p be the fraction of color 1. For adjacent layers of edge arrays (X,Y), define delta_XY=Pr[color(X_ij)!=color(Y_jk)] over independent uniform i,j,k. Define delta_YZ, delta_ZX cyclically. Write Delta for their sum.

**Expansion claim:** Delta>=2p(1-p). Take two independently uniformly sampled edge coordinates E,E' from all D, so disagreement probability is 2p(1-p). Conditional on their array labels, connect the two independent samples by a short sequence of edges in which consecutive edges are in different arrays and share their appropriate vertex:
- If both are X, E=X_ij and E'=X_i'j', choose independent uniform k, and use X_ij -> Y_jk -> Z_ki' -> X_i'j'. Each step is a uniformly sampled adjacent pair of its respective array types. Disagreement at endpoints implies disagreement on at least one step, hence conditional probability <=Delta.
- If the two arrays are different, say X and Y, use the third array as an intermediate: independent X_ij and Y_j'k connect via Z_ki. Each step again uniformly samples its adjacent pair type, so probability <=delta_ZX+delta_YZ<=Delta.
Rotate/reverse for other labels. Averaging over labels proves the claim. This is elementary union-bound expansion, no expander theorem or external rank lemma.

Thus some adjacent pair of arrays, say X,Y, has delta_XY>=2p(1-p)/3. For a permutation pi of [A], fix Z to its permutation matrix Z_(pi(i),i)=1 and all other Z entries zero. The triangle function becomes exactly
    OR_{i,j} (X_ij AND Y_(j,pi(i))).
There are A^2 disjoint pairs, because pi is bijective. Over uniformly random pi, the expected number of opposite-colored pairs is A^2 delta_XY (each pi(i) uniform). Therefore a permutation exists with at least
    r >= (2/3) A^2 p(1-p)
crossing pairs (integer ceil). Fix both entries of every noncrossing pair to zero. Every crossing pair retains two independently free bits u_l,v_l, with one on each side. After relabeling the exact cofactor is
    ND_r(u,v)=OR_{l=1}^r u_l v_l.
The cyclic array cases are identical. Only ordinary constant restrictions on original physical edge coordinates are used; fixing the third array is NOT a permutation of the input wiring. This theorem works on every bipartition, even when each array is itself split, and does not require balanced cuts until a numerical lower bound is desired.

## 3. Exact communication and directed ingress consequences

For r>=1 the disjointness matrix on Boolean u,v has entries product_l(1-u_l v_l), hence is the r-fold Kronecker power of [[1,1],[1,0]], an invertible matrix over the reals of rank 2^r. The matrix of ND_r is all-ones minus that matrix, hence rank at least 2^r-1 by subadditivity, and at most 2^r-1 because its all-zero u row is zero. Exact rank =2^r-1. There are also exactly 2^r distinct row residuals and 2^r distinct column residuals: testing a unit vector of the other side recovers every chosen bit. This is deterministic residual and real rank, not randomized communication complexity.

Let a live binary DAG compute the triangle slice; no monotonicity or AIG-specific gate restriction needed for these information arguments. Partition its designated physical edge inputs into S and T. Mark syntactic ancestry from S in the ORIGINAL live graph, and analogously T. The no-S side ports are the distinct underlying operand vertices of gates with one S-dependent and one S-independent operand. For the matching cofactor, all nonmatched inputs fixed, S side free bits u, T side free bits v. The no-S side tuple must distinguish all 2^r v assignments because given u and this tuple one can evaluate the S-dependent DAG topologically. So there are >=r distinct no-S side ports; analogously >=r no-T side ports.

More strongly in the ancestor-closed no-S subgraph, any vertex separator between the r matched T-side source bits and the no-S side ports supplies a code determining that port tuple (all other inputs are fixed). It must have >=r vertices by residual injectivity. Directed vertex Menger with unit source/sink capacity gives r vertex-disjoint paths from the matched T sources to distinct ports, extendable to distinct S-ingress gates by one edge. Similarly r vertex-disjoint paths in the no-T subgraph from matched S bits to distinct T-ingress gates. The same matching restriction witnesses BOTH requirements. Before their last edges these two path families are disjoint even from one another: every vertex on a path starting at a T source has T ancestry and no S ancestry, and conversely. They may meet at common final mixed ingress gates (seed gates). No claim of 2r distinct final gates or disjoint routes after entering the mixed region. This strengthens the per-slot routing certificate in a different direction: both directions hold for the same cofactor across every chosen data partition. Since the partition covers all designated edge coordinates, these are routes between the two pure regions and the mixed region, with fixed metadata inputs outside them. The theorem allows arbitrary nonedge inputs of the original SAT graph fixed to the triangle projection. Syntactic labels can be computed before or after the common restriction; do not infer reader counts of original graph from contraction without proof.

When p in [1/4,3/4], r>=A^2/8=D/24. Thus EVERY such partition has linear-size opposite-side ingress demand. More generally r>= (2/9)D p(1-p). This is not a sum of demands over all partitions; their paths and endpoints can heavily overlap.

## 4. Terminal separator corollary, without changing placement

Use the undirected underlying original circuit graph and give unit terminal weight to the D fixed physical triangle edge inputs. Suppose deletion of a vertex separator C leaves components each containing <=2D/3 terminals. If |C|>=D/48, already a linear separator. Otherwise group components into two sides A_side,B_side each containing >=D/4 terminals, by the same greedy argument as packed_separator_handoff.md. Give edge inputs in C either color for the bipartition. The matching theorem gives >=D/24 crossing pairs. Delete pairs with a terminal in C, losing at most |C| (pairs disjoint), leaving >=D/48 independent crossing pairs between the actual two sides. Fix all their unused inputs and all C inputs publicly as in the cofactor.

The standard topological circuit evaluation communication simulation from the earlier separator theorem costs <=2|C|+1 bits: each separator gate has at most two operands, and both parties exchange those values when needed, then announce output. All A-side and B-side gates can be evaluated locally once earlier C values known; no direct graph edges join the two sides. Accepting transcripts form disjoint rectangles, hence real rank <=2^(2|C|+1). By section 3, 2|C|+1>=log2(2^r-1)>=r-1, so |C|=Omega(D). This applies even after common hard metadata and nonedge payload coordinates have been fixed. Unlike the earlier packed unit-clause separator construction, the same single fixed triangle projection already has hard cofactors against every balanced partition, with no programmable tag reassignment or arbitrary physical role placement. The terminal set size is only Theta(n/log n), not Theta(n) as in the older packed-unit result. Thus this is a stronger fixed-subfamily property, NOT a numerical improvement on the previous full SAT separator bound.

Treewidth Omega(D) follows by the standard weighted tree-decomposition separator argument. The classical weighted planar and fixed-minor-free separator bounds cited in packed_separator_handoff.md give gate count Omega(D^2) in those architectures (on the triangle circuit, or original full circuit with n=Theta(D log D) primaries). In terms of the paired input length this is Omega(n^2/log^2 n). These are established graph-separator applications, not new graph theory or unrestricted lower bounds. Arbitrary DAGs may have sparse high-expansion graphs and evade a small-separator argument.

## 5. Genuine shared-witness problem, and limitations of the method

The triangle subfamily is no longer a single table lookup with externally supplied selected addresses: it existentially searches for compatible selections in all three simultaneous relations. A straightforward O(A^3) signed AIG computes the OR of all triple products. No claim of optimality, no unconditional superlinear ordinary gate lower bound here, and no claim that this subfamily has no quasilinear unrestricted circuits. Every permutation restriction in the proof itself has an O(A^2) circuit. Taking a maximum of their lower bounds or adding their independent ingress costs cannot establish a superlinear bound for the original graph.

The new bidirectional certificate permits shared seed gates and shared paths across different partitions. It does not yet constrain a wide-support gate's capacity enough to improve the ordinary unrestricted bound. A possible next step is to quantify simultaneous requirements for many partitions in a way that survives reconvergence, or use the three-way residual relation without reducing it to pairwise set intersection. In particular before any Z-dependent signal enters a data-only X,Y frontier, the frontier must encode the full Boolean matrix product: selecting Z as any unit matrix tests any entry, and Boolean matrix product ranges over every A-by-A Boolean matrix (fix X to identity). Hence >=A^2 frontier bits, an exact information demand but only linear in edge data. With mixed Z ingress code of q bits, cell counting using the invertible disjointness matrix shows c+q>=A^2: choose a code cell of >=2^(A^2-q) Z masks, whose ND residual columns are independent for all nonzero masks; use the DISJOINTNESS (complement) matrix, which includes the constant column for zero and is invertible. Restrict X=identity and vary Y, so the possible joint residual value vectors across that cell number at least its column rank, equal to cell size. Any common frontier map for that code must distinguish these patterns. This auxiliary bound is analytic, not a separate regression, and does not imply an efficient encoder or a superlinear charge. It illustrates the limit of scalar entropy alone.

## 2. Structural reduction of least lift-fixed circuits

Assume C computes F_G and is least, lift-fixed, with G nonempty. All gates live, no repeated operand gates (delete alias), no gate equivalent to an earlier available wire (redirect/delete). In particular any pure OR gate has at least two distinct singleton inputs. No raw input is BelowF since its singleton has no edge.
- A clean AND with a clean operand is redundant: replace its output by that operand everywhere. This can only increase output by monotonicity; on any negative input both the old gate and replacement are zero, so output remains zero. Positive inputs remain accepted. This yields a strictly smaller circuit in the exact model. Thus no AND (clean or dirty) can read a clean wire.
- A dirty OR cannot read a clean wire by lift fixed point. A clean OR has both operands clean by pointwise implication. Consequently the clean region is forward-closed, all its internal readers are ORs, and its sources are the clean ANDs. The output is clean, and its function is exactly OR of all clean AND outputs.
- If there are T clean ANDs, the clean OR collector has >=T-1 gates by connected binary source counting. It can be replaced by any OR tree over those T actual outputs using T-1 gates, with no change to dirty preprocessing or AND gates. Minimality forces equality, hence collector is a tree (including T=1, no collector gates). This is a syntactic and size statement in the model, not just a truth-table identity.
- Each gate must be essential on some minimal positive EDGE input under forced-zero intervention at that gate. Otherwise set it to zero, propagate zero through AND and OR, alias other operands, and prune; at least that gate disappears, no extra gates, the new monotone circuit is <=F and remains true at every edge input. Hence it computes F on all inputs. The temporary zero is eliminated before returning to IsAO2; final output is nonconstant for a nonempty graph. In particular every clean AND must own at least one edge that no other clean AND accepts. This holds even if its truth function includes higher minterms.
- More generally first dirty AND has only ORs/inputs in its ancestor cone: clean ancestors cannot reach the dirty region, and any earlier dirty AND would contradict earliest choice. In the exactly-one-junk case J, all dirty OR cones are ORs of raw inputs and possibly J; J's operands are nonempty pure disjunctions.

These elementary facts may overlap existing fleet results; no priority asserted. They reduce the exact one-junk statement to a much smaller residual normalization problem.

## 3. Exposed-centre lemma for a disjoint large rectangle

Suppose exactly one junk AND J=OR(A) AND OR(B), where A,B are disjoint sets of vertices and |A|,|B|>=2. The J gate necessarily dirty since a clean complete rectangle would contain a C4. For every clean AND K reading J through ORs, only one operand's OR closure can contain J: if both did, K>=J would be dirty. Its two operands therefore have form
    K=(J OR OR(D)) AND OR(C)
where D may be empty, C nonempty raw vertex set. D denotes only raw inputs in the J-containing operand, with no clean ancestors. If K reads J directly, D empty. This form holds as Boolean functions regardless of shared OR DAGs.

Because J AND OR(C)<=F, every a in A intersect C must be adjacent to EVERY vertex of B: on pair {a,b}, J and OR(C) are true. Symmetrically every b in B intersect C adjacent to ALL of A. By no C4, at most one vertex of A is universal into B and at most one of B universal into A, even across ALL readers C. Call such vertices exposed centres only if they occur in some reader C. At a graph edge input e, J is true iff it crosses A,B. Thus the edge pairs accepted through the J contribution at all readers are exactly the union of at most two stars:
    a AND OR(B) if exposed a exists;
    b AND OR(A) if exposed b exists.
These stars are genuine implicants on ALL inputs. C vertices outside A union B cannot help J accept any edge pair. The lemma concerns edge inputs, not equality of every reader on higher-weight inputs. On edge inputs distributing K into J*OR(C) OR OR(D)*OR(C) is exact. Removing J may lose some of those pairs, but never any other pair. If an edge already covered by surviving clean tops or raw contributions, it need not be rescued twice.

A separate overlap lemma: if |A intersect B|>=2 (not disjoint case), every such pure opposite reader C is contained in the common neighborhood of all intersection vertices, because J accepts their singleton inputs. No C4 implies all such C are subsets of a common set of size at most one. Thus all J-using clean AND readers have the same singleton opposite operand if any exist. This does not yet give a size-reducing rewrite; the case |A intersect B|=1 is also open here.

## 4. General exact deletion budget for disjoint large rectangle

Set J temporarily to zero, simplify, retaining its two original pure OR providers A,B if they are needed by new stars (do NOT charge their removal and reuse). Let b_removed be actual removed units, after any allowable collector pruning, excluding all retained providers. At least J and every distinct immediate reader of J are removed: an AND reading zero becomes zero, an OR reading zero aliases its other operand. Each original ID counted only once. No new gates in this part. The resulting monotone circuit is <=F and accepts all graph edge inputs except possibly the exposed-star edges. If the old output is zero, use only the new stars; otherwise OR new stars with old output. With k in {1,2} stars needed to cover remaining missing edges, added cost <=2k (or 2k-1 if old output zero). Each star requires one AND using a raw centre and an existing provider; collection costs at most k ORs. If no missing edges, simply delete J. If b_removed exceeds the actual added cost, original C cannot be least. In particular:
- one needed star and >=2 distinct immediate readers of J -> strict saving;
- two needed stars and >=4 immediate readers -> strict saving;
- equality only gives existence of a no-larger CLEAN circuit, NOT that the original least fixed point is clean.
This budget covers both direct AND readers and mixed OR readers but is not guaranteed sufficient for small fanout. We make no invalid global substitution J -> A or J -> B. E.g. P4 (double star a-b plus a-b2,a2-b) has F=(a OR a2)(b OR b2)(a OR b), a five-gate dirty circuit. Expanding J to either operand creates false singleton positives. A clean four-gate star cover exists.

## 5. Strict theorem: disjoint large rectangle and only direct AND readers

**Claim:** under the hypotheses in section 2, if exactly one junk J has disjoint pure operand supports A,B of cardinality >=2 and has NO immediate OR reader, contradiction. Equivalently, any remaining exactly-one-junk counterexample with disjoint large supports must have an immediate OR reader. This proves a special case of the actual L-meta-3754 one-junk hypothesis, not its full assertion.

Proof. Every J reader is a clean AND, with pure opposite C. Let t>=1 be their number. The clean output collector is an OR tree over all clean ANDs (section 2). Remove J and its t readers, retain providers A,B if needed. Replace the readers by at most k<=2 genuine exposed stars covering the missing edge inputs. Retain all other clean ANDs, including their providers. Rebuild the output collector as an OR tree; since the original had T clean ANDs, old collector T-1 and new collector T-t+k-1, for nonempty new list the net count change is <=2(k-t)-1. If k=0 all missing edges absent; nonempty graph implies some other clean AND survives. Thus strict improvement whenever k<=t. Only t=1,k=2 remains. Choose k minimally among the two exposed stars; each star must then contain at least one edge not covered by the other star and the surviving clean ANDs.

Write the unique reader K=J AND OR(C). Both exposed centres a in A and b in B belong to C. Since a universal to B and b universal to A, A and B are each homogeneous in the given bipartition and on opposite sides. C contains exactly one element of each A,B (no C4), and contains both colors. The root OR(C) has at least one gate. It cannot be used through ORs in an operand of any other clean AND: such an operand would contain both a,b, and any vertex in the other nonempty raw OR operand would have to be adjacent to both a,b, impossible in a bipartite graph (also singleton contradictions if overlap). All other ANDs except K and J have pure OR operands, since J has no OR reader. Nor can OR(C) feed either J operand, since neither A nor B contains both a,b. Every gate live and all dirty readers OR or among these ANDs. Hence OR(C)'s root has no uses outside K's cone that remain needed after removing K. Delete this root in addition to J,K.

We need save one further OR gate, and cannot blindly drop the central edge a-b from an OR provider that has shared subexpressions. Consider A's pure OR cone. Protect every gate in that cone lying in the cone of any other (surviving) clean AND. A protected OR node with support S subset A has |S|>=2 by leastness; if used by any other clean top, the opposite raw OR operand must contain only b: every vertex there must be a common neighbor of all S, and b is one such neighbor; no C4 gives uniqueness. Consequently every b-u edge for u in S is ALREADY accepted by the surviving clean ANDs. This holds if use is through ORs and the top operand has additional raw inputs. The A root itself cannot be protected, because k=2 minimal requires some b-A edge not covered by other tops or by the a-star. A-cone OR nodes cannot also feed B's provider or K's opposite OR(C), since their supports contain at least two A vertices while B disjoint and A intersect C={a}. All protected gates' ancestors in the A cone are protected as well.

Rebuild the EXCLUSIVE part of A's provider in place, treating the raw input a and every protected OR node whose support contains a as zero when they are read by an exclusive node. Leave all protected nodes unchanged for their other uses. Simplify OR with zero or duplicate operands, pruning unused exclusive gates. This can be viewed as a new earlier-wire ordering of a DAG; no cyclic dependence added. The resulting disjunction A' retains every A vertex u whose b-u edge is not already covered by surviving tops or by the a-star: the only dropped raw leaves are a (central edge covered by a-star) or belong to a dropped protected node (edges covered by surviving tops). It is nonempty by minimality of k=2. At least one exclusive OR gate disappears: follow a path from leaf a to exclusive root A; the first exclusive gate on that path reads either a itself or a protected gate containing a, which is replaced by zero. The exclusive root remains available in simplified form (possibly a single raw or protected wire). No new provider gates, all uses of the changed exclusive nodes outside their cone were only via the deleted J/reader; B and C cannot read them as argued. All original IDs accounted separately.

Now replace K locally by (a AND OR(B)) OR (b AND OR(A')), at cost three new gates (two AND, one OR), preserving the existing collector topology if desired. All new functions imply F. On every edge formerly accepted by K, either it remains covered by another top or it is covered by one of the two new stars. Other tops unchanged. Thus new output is exactly F by monotonicity and the edge-minterm property. Delete at least J,K,OR(C) root and one exclusive A OR gate, four distinct units for three new -> strictly smaller. This contradicts leastness, completing the claimed case. All temporary zeros and aliases eliminated; no constant gate needed in the final exact IsAO2 circuit.

## 6. What remains and proposed next steps

Remaining one-junk configurations include overlapping A,B (especially intersection size one), singleton operand(s), and disjoint large supports with an immediate OR reader and insufficient deletion budget. For the last case, the mixed OR region can be shared and the preserved raw contributions D*C matter. Need either a size-saving rewrite accounting for shared carriers or an actual counterexample to universal least-fixed cleanliness. Do not treat a nonminimal fixed point as a counterexample, or equal-size clean normalization as proof every least fixed point is clean. The many-junk case is still untouched; its nested dirty cones are a separate target. The above direct-reader theorem is proved on paper, not yet formalized in Lean. It may overlap fleet results not retrieved in this review. Next prioritize local formalization of section 2 and exposed-centre lemmas using exact definitions, then the strict shared-provider proof or a smaller formal decomposition. Match the current desk statement before any upstream submission. Existing C4-free native solver can be used as independent falsification controls; no production pruning rule added.

# Mixed-support asymmetry, dictionary ingress routing, and first-large-support compression

Date: 2026-11-26. Starting master 4471ac36090f32b0b2fe33afe52c673ddfad6ded. Extends [private compression](private_compression_handoff.md), [selector/dictionary frontier](selector_dictionary_frontier_handoff.md), [multiport residual reserve](multiport_residual_reserve_handoff.md), and [switchable incidence](switchable_incidence_handoff.md). Removes the private-compression premise from a weighted original-gate inequality, gives a stronger topology-dependent certificate using per-slot vertex-disjoint paths, and extends local compression charges to limited mixed-support regions. Still a restricted-architecture superlinear bound, not an unrestricted superlinear bound. Paper proofs and finite regression only; no independent audit, Lean verification, production change, priority claim or new physical encoding. L-clb-036 remains open.

## 1. Model and exact dictionary ingress

Use the already proved switchable physical SAT layout with t=3m slots of ell payload bits, P=2^ell. The unit-mode dictionary selector subfamily computes F(x,h,j)=h(x_j) on t independent x_i in [P], all P independent dictionary signs h, and all selector choices j. The metadata other than dictionary signs is fixed separately for each j. Every input of this subfamily is a legal SAT word. The physical payload and dictionary signs are distinct single-copy primary coordinates. No new packing/padding replay; the old grammar is used unchanged.

Take the live signed AIG (the support/count arguments in sections 1--4 also work for ordinary proper B2 gates), with free signs and constants, fixed original IDs. Designate all t slots; let sigma(v) be the subset of slots with at least one physical payload primary ancestor of vertex v. This is SYNTACTIC, not semantic support. Metadata-only vertices have sigma empty. For gate g with underlying operands u,v let
    a(g)=|sigma(u) symmetric_difference sigma(v)|.
The support union is sigma(g)=sigma(u) union sigma(v). Each gate with i in exactly one operand support has exactly one operand independent of slot i. Let D_i be the SET of distinct underlying independent side vertices at all such gates in the original live graph. Let I_i be the number of those gate occurrences (one per gate per slot).

Fix selector j=i and all other payloads to zero, keep x_i and all dictionary signs h free. The signals in D_i do not depend on x_i, so write their tuple as z_i(h). Every gate with i ancestry can be evaluated from x_i and z_i(h) in topological order: its operands either have i ancestry or are in D_i. Therefore if z_i(h)=z_i(h'), the residual functions of x_i are identical. Since those residuals are the full dictionary h and h' respectively, z_i is injective on all 2^P dictionary tables. Hence
    P <= |D_i| <= I_i.
In particular counting distinct source IDs is stronger than counting repeated fanout occurrences. Both counts are on original IDs before the different selector restrictions; actual simplification could remove or change support classes, but no such change is assumed here.

Double count pairs (i,g) with i in exactly one operand support:
    sum_g a(g)=sum_i I_i >= t P.
Metadata-only gates contribute zero. This is a weighted inequality on ONE original DAG, not an invalid sum of separate minimum circuit sizes. A gate may receive several slot charges, but its exact capacity is a(g), at most t. If there is a known upper A on all a(g), then B_D>=ceil(tP/A) (A=0 impossible). This does not require any bound on union support size: even gates with huge but nearly identical operand supports receive little charge. No claim that a large value of a(g) alone implies semantic sensitivity to every counted slot.

## 2. Necessity of dictionary-to-ingress routes, not just syntactic port padding

For each fixed i, take the induced sub-DAG on vertices with i NOT in sigma. This is ancestor closed. Let the sources be the P actual dictionary sign primaries, and sinks D_i. Other input primaries are constants under the selector-i restriction. Use unit vertex capacity, including both sources and sinks, and allow zero-length source-to-sink paths if a dictionary primary itself is a side port.

Any vertex separator K from those dictionary sources to D_i gives a code of at most |K| bits for the whole side tuple: evaluate the induced DAG in topological order treating K values as supplied, with all other primaries fixed; no path from a varying dictionary input can reach a sink without meeting K. Thus z_i(h) is determined by the values of K. Injectivity in section 1 forces |K|>=P. Vertex-capacitated max-flow/min-cut (equivalently directed vertex Menger, with vertex splitting and source/sink caps) gives P mutually vertex-disjoint directed paths from dictionary primaries to distinct side vertices. Every one of the P dictionary primaries participates. Extend each path by the side operand wire into one original i-dependent gate reading that side vertex. The extended endpoints can be chosen distinct: one gate counted for i has exactly one independent side operand, and all side vertices chosen by the flow are distinct. The paths up to the last edge are outside the i-dependent region. This is a per-slot routing theorem. Paths for DIFFERENT slots may share vertices or edges, including the dictionary sources themselves; no multicommodity constant-congestion theorem is asserted.

This certificate rejects a padded side set with >=P distinct gates whose dictionary-dependent ancestor cone has a smaller bottleneck. It is strictly more informative than the gate-asymmetry count. It is a necessary structural test (not a claim that feasible paths imply correct dictionary evaluation). It can use the original graph with unused primary values treated as constants, or a more refined actual selector restriction if certified; never infer semantic independence from sample evaluations alone.

## 3. First-large-support frontier with no private-region premise

For an integer 2<=r<=t, call a vertex low if |sigma(v)|<r, high otherwise. Low vertices form an ancestor-closed prefix; the output is high because all slots essential. Let G_low and G_high be the partition of data-dependent gates. For slot i let K_i be all distinct low vertices with i in sigma read by high gates, including raw primaries when appropriate. These are i-dependent ports at the first-large-support frontier. Every path from i to output meets K_i, and p_i=|K_i|>=1. All low i-dependent gates read either an earlier low i-dependent operand or a low independent side vertex. Let D_i^lo be the distinct independent side sources of these low gates; I_i^lo the corresponding gate occurrence count. Fix j=i, other payloads zero, let h vary as above.

A fixed code of the D_i^lo signals determines a fixed map x_i -> K_i values. For any dictionary in this code cell, the rest of the circuit computes h(x_i) via this map and independent signals. Thus every dictionary in the cell is constant on the fibers of the same map. At most 2^p_i image values are possible. When p_i<ell, the exact nonconstant-dictionary cover count from private_compression_handoff.md applies: at most 2^(2^p_i)-2 nonconstant dictionaries per cell. There are 2^P-2 nonconstant dictionaries, and the number of cells <=2^|D_i^lo|. Hence
    |D_i^lo| >= L_i = P-2^p_i+1  if 1<=p_i<ell,
    L_i=0 otherwise.
For p_i>=ell this floor is only the trivial one, not a claim zero ingress is sufficient in that topology. p_i=0 impossible by essentiality. The argument does not require D_i^lo to consist of metadata-only signals with respect to ALL slots: after other payloads are fixed, any no-i-ancestry signal is a function of h, and all relevant ports are retained. It also does not require the maps for different i to coexist on one fixed h or selector.

As in section 2, every dictionary-source-to-D_i^lo vertex separator inside the no-i-ancestry low subgraph gives a code determining the port map. Apply the same nonconstant count to the separator values. Thus its minimum vertex cut is at least L_i; there exist at least L_i vertex-disjoint paths within that region from distinct dictionary primaries to distinct side sources, extendable to distinct low ingress gates for this i. Again no simultaneous across-slot disjointness is inferred.

Double count low pairs:
    sum_(g low) a(g)=sum_i I_i^lo >= sum_i L_i.
Each low gate a(g)<=|sigma(g)|<=r-1. If Z_r slots have p_i<ell, then
    (r-1)|G_low| >= sum_i L_i >= Z_r (P/2+1).
This extends the disjoint private result (r=2) to arbitrarily mixed low-support gates, with the explicit overlap capacity r-1. At r=2 the low gates are private and each capacity <=1. At larger r no claim that slot regions are disjoint; the weighted bound accounts for all their overlaps.

## 4. Disjoint low/high reserve and small-circuit consequences

Define I_i^hi=I_i-I_i^lo. These are counts on the DISJOINT sets of low and high original gate IDs. From section 1
    sum_(g high) a(g)=sum_i I_i^hi >= sum_i max(0,P-I_i^lo).
If actual low side-source sets are available, the sharper floor is max(0,P-|D_i^lo|) for number of DISTINCT new high side sources, since |D_i|>=P and D_i is union of low and high side sets. It also lower bounds high occurrences. The routing theorem gives P disjoint paths per slot; if at most b distinct low ingress endpoints occur, at least P-b of the chosen paths must end at high ingress gates. Do not subtract a lower bound for low cost from the global lower bound: use actual low counts or an upper bound.

More generally split gates by asymmetry rather than support union. For a threshold A>=1 let low-asymmetry gates have a(g)<=A, and wide gates a(g)>A. If B_D=s then sum_i low-asym ingress occurrences <=As. Hence at most 2As/P slots can have >=P/2 low-asym ingress occurrences. All other slots require >=P/2 distinct wide ingress gates (and at least P/2 of the P disjoint paths in section 2 end at wide gates). Each wide gate can serve at most t slots, so if As=o(tP), at least (1-o(1))P/2 distinct wide gates are required, or directly sum a(g) implies >=(1-o(1))P such gates using total wide mass divided by t. Distinguish these numerical conclusions: nearly every slot needs many wide endpoints, but a collection of only O(P) such gates can serve them all.

Use m=2^k, t=3m, ell=floor(alpha*k), 0<alpha<=1, P=Theta(m^alpha), n=Theta(m log m) in the existing switchable grammar. If a(g)<=O(m^beta) for every gate, then
    s=Omega(m^(1+alpha-beta))
     =Omega((n/log n)^(1+alpha-beta)).
For alpha=1/4 and beta<0.24 this is above n^1.01 asymptotically. This is a restricted-architecture theorem, but its premise is different from private compression and allows early mixing.

Conversely suppose s=O(n^(1+epsilon)), epsilon<alpha. Choose any fixed delta>0 with alpha-epsilon-delta>0 and threshold A=floor(m^(alpha-epsilon-delta)). Then
    As/(tP)=O(m^(-delta)(log m)^(1+epsilon))=o(1).
Almost every slot requires at least P/2 distinct wide-asymmetry ingress gates, even if it was not privately compressed. These gates must connect operands whose slot supports differ by more than A, not simply have large union support. For alpha=.25, epsilon=.01, A=m^.20 is an illustrative choice (delta=.04). The first-large-support variant with r=1+floor(m^(alpha-epsilon-delta)) yields
    Z_r/t <= (r-1)s/[t(P/2+1)] = o(1).
Therefore almost every slot must expose at least ell distinct i-dependent frontier signals BEFORE its information enters a gate with >=r slot ancestors. This is a family of topology constraints at intermediate support scales, not only the first private/mixed boundary. Multiple slots can share the same frontier signal, and the theorem does NOT show that those signals are jointly injective on all slots under a fixed metadata value.

The full switchable SAT promise also gives the previous pair-complete global ledger B_D>=2D-2+ceil(log2 R), and the earlier private-compression bound. Use the MAX of distinct valid total floors unless a single exact resource ledger proves disjointness. In particular the wide-asymmetry gate charge might overlap all existing gate charges. The low/high sum above is justified because it counts disjoint gate sets in the SAME weighted ledger.

## 5. Explicit upper controls and remaining barrier

The selector-first circuit selects an ell-bit address from all slots and evaluates h on that address once. It costs O(t ell+P) gates on the dictionary-selector promise (with appropriate selector controls), and can expose ell private ports per slot while using high-asymmetry gates in the shared decoder. The dictionary-first circuit costs O(tP) and compresses each slot to one private signal. Hybrids interpolate. All are correct for the simple selector promise only, not arbitrary 3-CNF in hard mode; none is evidence for a small full SAT circuit. The weighted theorem explains precisely how selector-first sharing avoids the costly local architecture. High asymmetry is syntactic and may be generated by large merged cones; counting large support does not itself establish expensive semantic mixing. Per-slot disjoint routes can all reuse the same dictionary wires and later wide gates. Neither adding their path lengths across i nor charging every slot separately at a wide gate is sound. No theorem established that hard-mode incidence forces enough additional cost to exceed the capacity t of a wide gate.

## 7. Next research step

The key escape route is now precisely described: an O(n^1.01) signed AIG must preserve many per-slot signals up to growing support thresholds and route dictionary information to many wide-asymmetry gates. Shared wide endpoints can serve many slots. To push further, seek a hard-mode property that forces distinct or limited-capacity wide endpoints after fixing metadata, or a cross-context certificate that rules out the shared decoder arrangement without assuming private compression. The present result does not establish such a property. Keep the weighted and mincut certificates available for candidate topology analysis; no production pruning changes or speedup claims. Independent audit and Lean proof outstanding. L-clb-036 remains open.
